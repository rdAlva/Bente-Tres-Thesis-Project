Social Commerce Platforms and Their Role in Precipitating Unplanned 
Acquisitions Among Urban Working Adults 
Abstract 
Whether a measurable correlation exists between product promotion via social networking platforms and 
unplanned purchasing behavior constitutes the central inquiry of this investigation. A descriptive 
quantitative framework was adopted to characterize the prevailing conditions of the variables under 
examination. Self-administered survey instruments, structured around a Likert-type scale, were distributed 
to a total of 387 employed respondents drawn from Angeles City, Pampanga. Pearson r correlation and 
mean rating computations were applied to the dataset to interrogate the hypothesized association between 
social commerce activity and spontaneous buying conduct. A statistically significant relationship was 
identified between impulsive acquisition tendencies among young professionals and the hedonic as well 
as social dimensions of social commerce. Among the recommendations advanced by the researchers are: 
the reinforcement of trustworthiness and credibility within social commerce ecosystems, the institution of 
sales promotion mechanisms to sustain consumer engagement, and the elevation of service quality 
standards to enrich customer experiences and deliver enhanced value. These results underscore the 
imperative for continued scholarly inquiry into the broader constellation of factors that shape social 
commerce behavior. 
Keywords: Social Commerce, Hedonic Value, Utilitarian Value, Impulse Buying Decision. 
 
Introduction and Review of Related Literature 
Every domain of contemporary human existence has been penetrated by social media. Having emerged as 
an indispensable societal instrument, it facilitates connectivity and interpersonal engagement across vast 
distances. Given the accelerated pace of technological progress, it is entirely unsurprising that the 
purchasing habits of consumers have undergone commensurate transformation. What was once purely a 
platform for social interaction has been reconstituted as a vehicle for marketing and commercial exchange 
— a phenomenon designated as Social Commerce. Defined by Dollarhide (2020), social commerce 
encompasses the leveraging of social networking platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter for 
the promotion and commercial distribution of goods and services. 
The phenomenon of impulsive purchasing conduct has attracted sustained attention from both 
practitioners and scholars across the globe. Retail environments, together with the spontaneous urges they 
trigger, exert a dual influence on consumer acquisition behavior. Research by Akram et al. (2016) 
establishes that, in the course of making purchasing decisions, consumers are predominantly swayed by 
the primary commodities and services placed before them. Per the observations of Saad et al. (2015), 
impulsive buying constitutes a pervasive and consequential challenge within the retail sector and 
marketing discipline — one that accounts for a substantial proportion of annual sales volume across 
numerous industry categories. 
As documented by Khem et al. (2018), information encountered within digital reviews exerts a 
measurable influence on consumers' online acquisition behavior. The hedonic gratification derived from 
engaging with internet-based reviews was found to amplify browsing intensity among consumers. In turn, 

heightened browsing propensity exerts a positive effect on impulsive purchasing willingness, thereby 
stimulating spontaneous buying acts. An additional distinction is drawn between consumer types: those 
characterized by elevated impulsiveness assign greater weight to the hedonic dimensions of online 
reviews, whereas those with diminished impulsiveness tend to privilege utilitarian considerations. For the 
highly impulsive consumer segment, the act of browsing is found to exert a disproportionately stronger 
influence on their readiness to engage in spontaneous purchases. 
The overarching objective of the study is to ascertain whether a demonstrable linkage exists between 
social commerce and impulse purchasing behavior. Of particular interest to the research team are the 
demographic characteristics of respondents alongside the association between discrete social commerce 
attributes and impulse purchase decisions, as mediated by hedonic and utilitarian value orientations. 
 
Methods 
Research Design 
A descriptive quantitative research design was employed by the investigative team. Quantitative data 
capturing the spontaneous purchasing behavior of young professionals residing in Angeles City formed 
the empirical foundation of the study. A survey-based methodology was adopted as the primary 
instrument for data collection, enabling the researchers to address the study's guiding questions and fulfill 
its stated objectives. 
Participants of the Study 
Purposive sampling was the selection strategy employed to identify an appropriate respondent pool. A 
total of 387 participants was determined to constitute a sufficient sample size for the study. The deliberate 
focus of the investigation was directed toward millennial or Generation Y employees — specifically, 
working individuals between 23 and 35 years of age — drawn from Angeles City, with their perspectives, 
attitudes, and experiential accounts serving as the primary source of inquiry. 
Data Gathering 
The survey questionnaire functioned as the principal data collection instrument. A Likert scale structure 
was provided to enable respondents to register their responses along a graduated continuum. The 
dissemination of survey instruments to individual respondents across Angeles City was accomplished via 
Google Forms, distributed through Facebook Messenger, consistent with a purposive sampling strategy. 
Procedure for Data Processing and Analysis 
An evaluative process was applied to the dataset to determine the validity of the research hypotheses. 
Research questions were addressed through descriptive statistical analysis, which synthesized data 
through both narrative and numerical modalities. The Likert scale served as the analytical scaffolding 
through which the research challenge was operationalized. Mean Rating and Pearson-R Correlation were 
the two statistical procedures deployed for analytical purposes. While the former computes a weighted 
average reflective of the relative importance of sampled items, the latter establishes both the existence and 
magnitude of the association between any two given variables.

Results and Discussion 

Demographic analysis of the sample revealed a distribution of 127 male respondents (32.8 percent) and 
260 female respondents (67.2 percent). Among the income brackets examined, the stratum corresponding 
to a monthly income range of 11,691 to 23,381 registered the highest concentration of respondents at 147 
(38.0 percent), while the bracket encompassing earners at 46,762 and above recorded the lowest 
representation at 55 respondents (14.2 percent). 
With respect to hedonic value, the item asserting that browsing social commerce satisfies one's curiosity 
achieved the highest mean rating of 3.36, corresponding to a descriptive classification of Strongly Agree. 
Conversely, the item pertaining to feelings of satisfaction and relaxation derived from social commerce 
browsing registered the lowest mean score of 3.01, classified as Agree. The aggregate mean for Hedonic 
Value across the instrument was determined to be 3.18, rated as Agree. 
Turning to utilitarian value, the statement addressing the exploratory behavior on social commerce 
platforms — specifically Facebook, Instagram, or Twitter — on account of the utility of the products or 
services available therein, was identified as bearing the highest utilitarian significance. The item affirming 
that social commerce exploration is motivated by the prospect of extracting greater value from the listed 
offerings registered the highest mean of 3.41 (Strongly Agree), while the item yielding the lowest mean 
score of 3.06 fell within the Agree descriptive band. The overall mean for Utilitarian Value was 3.18, 
corresponding to Agree. 
Regarding impulse buying behavior, the statement linking social commerce app browsing to desires for 
items outside one's predetermined purchasing agenda was found to be associated with impulsive 
acquisition tendencies. The item pertaining to occasional irresponsible purchasing conduct while 
browsing social commerce secured the highest mean of 2.95 (Agree), whereas the lowest-rated item in the 
construct registered a mean of 2.65 (Agree). The composite mean for Impulse Buying Decision was 2.75, 
categorized under Agree. 
Statistical testing produced a p-value of 0.001, which falls below the established significance threshold of 
0.05, thereby confirming the existence of a significant relationship between the hedonic dimension of 
social commerce and impulsive buying behavior. The nature of this relationship was characterized as 
weakly positive in direction. 
An analogous pattern emerged in the analysis of utilitarian value: a p-value of 0.001, likewise below the 
0.05 alpha level, established the statistical significance of the association between social commerce's 
utilitarian dimension and impulsive purchase decisions. This relationship was similarly identified as 
exhibiting a slight positive orientation.

Conclusion 

This investigation examined the degree to which social commerce activity is associated with impulsive 
purchasing tendencies among the young professional demographic of Angeles City. The analytical 
framework incorporated hedonic value, utilitarian value, and demographic variables — namely biological 
sex and monthly income — as explanatory components underlying spontaneous purchasing conduct. The 
principal conclusions drawn from the evidence are as follows. 
A pronounced gender asymmetry was observed: female respondents constituted the dominant portion of 
the sample at 260 individuals (67.2 percent), while male respondents numbered 127 (32.8 percent). This 
finding aligns with Pradhan's (2016) contention that female consumers exhibit impulsive buying behavior 
with greater frequency, a tendency attributed to their heightened susceptibility and more rapid exposure to 
merchandise. 
Among income-based demographic segments, the highest earning bracket — monthly incomes of 46,762 
and above — was represented by the fewest respondents, comprising only 55 individuals or 14.2 percent 
of the total. This pattern is consistent with the interpretation that higher-earning young professionals 
demonstrate comparatively lower susceptibility to impulsive purchasing. As articulated by Akram et al. 
(2018), the inverse relationship between income magnitude and impulsive buying frequency suggests that 
affluence functions as a moderating variable. The lowest mean rating in the hedonic dimension — 3.01 
for the item concerning satisfaction and relaxation during social commerce browsing — reflects a degree 
of consumer unease attributable to the absence of guarantees that expectations will be met, potentially 
giving rise to confusion or dissatisfaction. 
In the utilitarian dimension, the item related to extracting enhanced value from products promoted 
through social commerce received the lowest mean rating of 3.06 (Agree). Given that social commerce 
sellers are predominantly small-scale entrepreneurs, this finding suggests that the perceived value offered 
by digitally promoted goods and services falls short relative to conventional retail outlets. Taken together, 
the data indicate that while both hedonic and utilitarian social commerce values maintain statistically 
significant associations with impulsive purchasing decisions, these associations are comparatively weaker 
than those observed in physical retail contexts. The encounter with a product in a digital environment 
appears to attenuate the spontaneous purchasing impulse — consumers may engage with the listing out of 
curiosity without translating that curiosity into a purchase act. 
 
Recommendations 
The accuracy and honesty of product and service information presented through social commerce 
channels must be ensured in order to foster consumer comfort and satisfaction, thereby minimizing the 
risk of deception. To cultivate perceptions of reliability and engender consumer confidence, vendors are 
encouraged to present authentic, unenhanced product imagery rather than digitally manipulated 
representations. The provision of discount incentives to purchasers, coupled with the solicitation of 
comment-based feedback through coupon mechanisms, would serve a dual function: generating a 
repository of consumer experience reviews and establishing a credibility benchmark for prospective 
buyers. Given that product and service quality is frequently gauged through peer-generated evaluations, 
such mechanisms hold significant potential for building trust with target audiences. The researchers 
further advocate the introduction of conditional free shipping offers above a designated purchase 
threshold — a logistical value-add — alongside enhancements to product packaging designed to 
emphasize the most salient attributes of the offerings available through Social Commerce. Consumer 
research indicates that packaging distinctiveness is a meaningful determinant of product trial behavior. 
Direct communication channels between buyers and sellers are recommended, with vendors expected to 
deliver prompt, responsive service. It is through the consistent delivery of superior customer service that 
enduring relationships of trust and loyalty between consumers and sellers are most effectively cultivated. 

Concurrently, consumers are encouraged to adopt a reflective and discerning posture when navigating 
social commerce environments — specifically, to consult existing customer evaluations as a basis for 
assessing product or service reliability and to contribute their own post-purchase assessments in turn. As a 
methodological recommendation for future research, comparable studies should be undertaken to 
consolidate and extend these findings. Future sampling strategies should seek to achieve a more balanced 
demographic distribution — particularly with respect to sex, age, and gender — so as to yield more 
nuanced and representative insights into which consumer segments are most predisposed to impulsive 
buying within social commerce environments.
