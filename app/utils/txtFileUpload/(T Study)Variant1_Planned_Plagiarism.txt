Post-Graduation Outcomes of Bachelor of Science in 
Accountancy Completers (2014–2019): 
An Institutional Tracer Investigation at Systems Plus College 
Foundation 
Gian Carlo N. Catahum 
Abstract 
Conceived as a diagnostic instrument for identifying the disconnect between institutional 
output and labor market demand, this investigation examined the workforce integration 
outcomes of BSA graduates produced by SPCF during the six-year span from 2014 
through 2019. Central to the inquiry were three interconnected concerns: (1) the extent 
to which the academic curriculum and departmental practices prepared graduates for 
professional roles; (2) the competencies developed during coursework that 
demonstrably contributed to securing and sustaining employment; and (3) the overall 
satisfaction level graduates held toward their current occupational conditions. 
Participation was secured from 94 of the 103 qualifying graduates within the target 
cohort. Administered through a collaborative network comprising alumni, instructors, 
enrolled students, and student leadership bodies, the data collection effort extended to 
social media channels to maximize outreach. Gathered responses were subjected to 
percentage-based frequency analysis and organized into tabular form for systematic 
interpretation. 
The findings established that 89.4% of participating graduates had successfully secured 
employment. A notable proportion of these graduates affirmed that the curriculum 
structure, departmental study program, and BSA-aligned experiential activities proved 
operationally applicable to their professional roles. It was further determined that 
referral-based recruitment networks continued to function as the predominant and 
effective channel through which graduates obtained initial employment. 
Additionally, the investigation yielded recommendations for enhancing graduate 
competitiveness. Identified areas requiring institutional reinforcement include: 
entrepreneurial aptitude, oral and written communication proficiency, analytical 
problem-solving and judgment skills, foreign language capability, and information 
communication technology (ICT) literacy. Formalization of partnership agreements with 
industrial establishments concentrated in Angeles City and the broader Pampanga 
region was strongly advocated. 
 
Chapter 1: Introduction 

Aspiring toward the status of a globally competitive academic institution in the domains 
of instruction, scholarly inquiry, and community engagement, SPCF undertook this 
investigation to illuminate for institutional leaders and academic staff the current 
employment circumstances of graduates from the BSA Department. The outcomes 
generated by this inquiry served as a substantive analytical resource for both the 
institution and its current and former students. 
Specifically, the study charted a developmental trajectory aimed at synchronizing 
academic program offerings with the evolving expectations of the labor sector. It served 
as an institutional awakening — directing attention toward areas where resource 
allocation and student support services required enhancement, particularly in light of the 
increasingly technologically driven and internationally competitive employment 
environment. 
The analytical outcomes of this study informed the crafting of evidence-based curricular 
strategies and pedagogical methodologies attuned to actual industry needs, which are 
subject to rapid and unpredictable shifts in specification and expectation. 
 
Chapter 2: Problem of the Study and Objectives 
Central to this inquiry was the overarching question: To what degree had SPCF BSA 
graduates from the period 2014 to 2019 been successfully absorbed into the 
professional workforce? Beyond the employability rate itself, the study sought empirical 
data capable of informing the institution regarding the overall functional impact of the 
BSA academic program, its curricular framework, and its instructional methodologies. Of 
equal interest was the current professional circumstance of each graduate. 
Insight derived from this investigation was positioned to directly support curriculum 
review and revision processes, ultimately elevating the knowledge, capabilities, and 
practical skills of future students in accordance with what the industry and labor market 
actually demand — thereby improving the probability of graduate employment 
outcomes. 
Study Objectives 
The following specific objectives guided the investigation: 
a. To quantify the proportion of SPCF graduates who had attained employment versus 
those who remained unemployed; 
b. To identify and analyze the underlying causes of graduate unemployment; 
c. To establish the typical duration between graduation and initial job placement; 
d. To trace and categorize the primary channels through which graduates identified and 
secured employment; 

e. To examine the motivational factors behind graduates' decisions to remain in their 
current employment; 
f. To ascertain which competencies cultivated during academic training were perceived 
by graduates as having the greatest applied utility; and 
g. To determine the extent to which departmental activities were translated into actual 
professional practice. 
 
Chapter 3: Research Methodology 
Sampling Plan 
The target population from which respondents were drawn consisted entirely of 
graduates from the BSA Department of SPCF for the cohort years 2014 through 2019. 
Sample Size 
A total of 112 individuals graduated from the BSA Department within the defined period. 
Because the intent was to achieve complete population coverage, total enumeration 
was applied as the sampling approach — that is, every graduate was considered 
eligible and targeted as a respondent. 
Research Instrument and Data Collection Procedure 
A purpose-built survey questionnaire was developed and calibrated to ensure that its 
items were accessible and interpretable across the range of respondents. Institutional 
cooperation was solicited from multiple offices and units, including the Office of the 
Registrar, the External Affairs Office, the Research and Development Office, the Alumni 
Office, and the College of Business Dean's Office, in addition to active student 
organizations with established ties to program graduates. 
Faculty members from within the BSA Department were enlisted as research 
supervisors. Currently enrolled students, particularly members of the departmental 
student organization, were recruited and trained as field enumerators for systematic 
data collection. Instrument deployment commenced at the beginning of the First 
Semester of Academic Year 2019-2020, following instrument pre-testing and validation. 
Retrieval of completed responses concluded in late August, with the formal presentation 
of findings delivered prior to the close of the same semester. 
Statistical Treatment 
All collected data were compiled, organized into frequency tables, and processed 
through percentage-based statistical analysis. Results were rendered visually through 
tabular formats and pie chart representations to facilitate clarity in interpretation. 
 

Chapter 4: Results and Discussion 
The data generation process was intensive and multi-stakeholder, drawing upon 
research students, feasibility study participants, Junior Philippine Institute of 
Accountants (JPIA) leaders, and faculty members who worked to reestablish 
communication links with former students. Alumni were also engaged as network 
facilitators during the collection process. 
Out of 103 graduates who constituted the reference cohort, 94 individuals submitted 
valid responses — representing an overall response rate of 83.9%. Among male 
graduates, 28 of 33 participated (84.84%), while 66 of 79 female graduates responded 
(83.54%). 
Number of Graduates and Participants 
Male graduates numbered 33 (29.36% of total), with 28 participating (84.84%). Female 
graduates numbered 79 (70.64%), with 66 participating (83.54%). Total respondents: 94 
out of 112, representing 83.92%. 
A. Gender Distribution 
Among the 94 respondents who completed the administered questionnaire, 28 were 
male (29.8%) and 66 were female (70.2%). This distribution confirms a predominance of 
female completers within the department for the reference period spanning 2011 to 
2014. 
B. Employment Status of Graduates 
Data extracted from the survey revealed that 84 respondents (89.4%) were actively 
engaged in paid employment at the time of data collection. The remaining 10 
respondents (10.6%) fell into categories including unemployment, self-employment, or 
individuals deferring employment in anticipation of international work placement. This 
outcome reflects a substantially high employment absorption rate within the BSBA 
Department graduate pool. 
C. Job Posting 
Of the 94 respondents, 62 (66%) confirmed that their current position represented their 
initial post-graduation employment. The remaining 34% had progressed to second or 
subsequent employment placements, indicating occupational progression among a 
notable subset of graduates. 
D. Time Spent Before Landing a Job 
Employment acquisition was achieved within the six-month window for 49 graduates 
(52.1%), making this the modal timeframe for job entry. An additional 28.7% secured 
employment within a year, while 10.6% required up to two years. Only 5.3% managed 
placement within the first month, and 3.2% — a minimal proportion — did not obtain 
employment until beyond two years post-graduation. 

E. Source of Job 
The dominant job acquisition pathway was through referral networks, encompassing 
connections affiliated with SPCF — including former faculty, classmates, alumni, 
relatives, and friends — accounting for 51.1% (48 respondents). Walk-in applications 
accounted for 18.1% (17 respondents), while 16% (15 respondents) utilized digital 
platforms and internet-based search tools. Job fair attendance and agency placement 
were reported by 10.6% and 4.3%, respectively. 
F. Reason of Unemployment 
A dominant 89.4% of the 84 graduates who were actively employed indicated that 
unemployment was not applicable to their situation. Among the 10 respondents 
experiencing unemployment: three (3.2%) cited absence of professional networks, 
inadequate starting compensation, and failure to meet professional licensure 
prerequisites; two reported family obligations as prohibitive factors; two identified 
insufficient work experience as their principal barrier; and only one graduate attributed 
unemployment to a perceived absence of available positions. 
G. Relevance of the Curriculum on the First Job 
An overwhelming 93.6% (88 graduates) affirmed that their academic curriculum was 
applicable and relevant to the demands encountered in their initial professional role. 
The six graduates (6.4%) who disagreed were predominantly those engaged in 
overseas employment, specifically in maritime or healthcare service capacities — fields 
with limited alignment to the accountancy program's core competencies. 
H. Competencies in College that are Useful on their Job 
Course-specific professional competencies — encompassing customer service, retail 
operations, sales proficiency, and client relationship management — were identified as 
most applicable by 40 respondents (42.6%). Analytical aptitude encompassing 
problem-solving and critical thinking was ranked second, cited by 26 participants 
(27.7%). Interpersonal and human relations skills were identified by 15 respondents 
(16%), followed by verbal and written communication at 9.6%. Entrepreneurial skill sets 
were noted by 4.3% of the cohort. 
I. BSBA Related Activities Practiced at Job 
When respondents were queried about the departmental activities most actively applied 
in their professional roles, marketing and sales operations emerged as the foremost 
domain, cited by exactly half (50%) of participants. Customer relations management 
followed at 23.4%. Research and data collection activities were cited by 17 respondents 
(18.1%), while office procedures management and human resource administration were 
each reported by 4.3%. 
J. Location of the First Job 

Geographic concentration of initial employment was pronounced in the Angeles City 
and surrounding area — including the Clark Export Processing Zone — which was 
named by 60 respondents (63.8%). Employment within the broader Pampanga province 
accounted for 23.4% (22 respondents), while 5.3% were placed elsewhere in the 
Central Luzon region. Three respondents were based in Metro Manila, with the 
remaining 4.3% reporting placement in distant or international locations. 
K. Abilities and Capabilities Performed on their Job 
K.1. Workplace Adaptability 
A majority of 54.3% (51 respondents) rated their capacity for workplace adaptability at 
the highest possible level. An additional 44% reported above-average adaptability, while 
only 4.3% (4 respondents) indicated a considerably low level of adaptive capacity in 
their employment environment. 
K.2. Problem Solving and Decision Making Skills 
Assessment of analytical competencies revealed that 31.9% (30 respondents) rated 
their proficiency as good, with 28.7% (27 respondents) registering a very good rating 
and 10.6% achieving the excellent tier. Of the remaining respondents, 20.2% (19 
individuals) assessed their capability as satisfactory, while 8.5% (8 individuals) gave 
themselves a poor rating in this domain. 
K.3. Confidence 
Self-reported confidence in professional task execution was rated very good by 50% (47 
respondents), making it the modal response category. Excellent and good ratings were 
recorded by 18.1% and 19.1% of respondents, respectively. A fair confidence level was 
reported by 7.4%, with only 5.3% describing their professional self-assurance as poor. 
K.4. Working in a Team 
The majority of respondents (53.2%, or 50 individuals) rated their collaborative 
team-working capability as excellent. A very good rating was recorded by 26.6% (25 
respondents), and good teamwork capacity was acknowledged by 11.7%. The lowest 
ratings — fair and poor — were represented by 6.4% and 2.1% of the cohort, 
respectively. 
K.5. Communication Skills 
An exact half of the surveyed population (50%, or 47 respondents) rated their 
communicative competency at the excellent level. Good performance was recorded by 
22.3% (21 respondents), very good by 14.9% (14 respondents), fair by 7.4%, and poor 
by 5.3%. 
K.6. Language Proficiency (Written English and Filipino) 
Language proficiency across written English and Filipino modalities was rated excellent 
by 35 respondents (35.1%) and very good by 28.7%. The remaining respondents 
distributed across good (19.1%), fair (10.6%), and poor (6.4%), indicating an overall 

favorable — though heterogeneous — level of linguistic capability among the graduate 
cohort. 
K.7. Usage of ICT 
In the domain of information and communication technology application, 31.9% of 
respondents assessed their capabilities at the excellent tier, while 31 individuals (33%) 
rated themselves as very good ICT users. Good proficiency was acknowledged by 14 
respondents (14.9%). Fair and poor ICT capability were cited by 11.7% and 8.5%, 
respectively. 
L. Evaluation and Satisfaction on the Current Job 
L.1. Valuable Experience 
When assessed on the degree to which their current job had provided substantive 
experiential learning, 47.9% of respondents (the single largest response group) 
described the experience as very good. Excellent experiential value was affirmed by 
25.5% (24 respondents), while 13.8% rated it as good. The remaining respondents 
rated this dimension as fair (8.5%) or poor (4.3%). 
L.2. Challenging 
The degree of professional challenge embedded in respondents' current occupational 
roles was rated very good by 48.9% (46 respondents), and excellent by 27.7% (26 
respondents). A good challenge level was reported by 11.7%, with 7.4% and 4.3% 
selecting fair and poor, respectively. 
L.3. Many Things Can be Learned 
Regarding the learning potential inherent in their current professional positions, 41.5% 
(39 respondents) assigned an excellent rating, and 34% (32 respondents) rated it as 
very good. Good, poor, and very poor ratings were recorded by 14.9%, 6.4%, and 3.2%, 
respectively. 
L.4. Relevance of the Study Program 
Perceptions of the study program's relevance to current occupational roles were highly 
favorable: 33% rated it excellent and 41.5% assessed it as very good. The good 
category was selected by 17% of respondents, while fair and poor assessments were 
recorded by 6.4% and 2.1%, respectively. 
L.5. Self-Satisfaction 
With respect to personal occupational satisfaction, 50% (47 respondents) indicated a 
very good level of contentment with their current role. Excellent self-satisfaction was 
reported by 29.8% (28 respondents). Good, fair, and poor levels of satisfaction were 
reflected in ratings of 11.7%, 5.3%, and 3.2%, respectively. 
L.6. Extent the Study Program Helpful in the Current Job 

Assessment of the academic study program's overall functional utility in current 
employment showed that 48.9% of respondents (46 individuals) rated its helpfulness as 
excellent — the modal category. A further 34% found it very good, and 9.6% considered 
it good. Fair helpfulness was cited by 6.4%, while a single respondent (1.1%) indicated 
poor utility. 
 
Chapter 5: Conclusion and Recommendation 
Drawing from the responses of 94 participants — representing 83% of the complete 
BSBA graduate cohort for the 2011–2014 period — it was established that 89.4% (84 
graduates) had been successfully integrated into the workforce or were currently 
employed at the time of the study. The retention of 66% of graduates in their original 
employment placement further underscores the notable degree of occupational 
satisfaction maintained among the group. 
Employment attainment within six months of graduation was confirmed by over 52% of 
respondents, and nearly 30% secured work before the one-year mark was reached. The 
overall employability of the cohort was therefore characterized as very high. Referral 
systems — particularly those tied to SPCF's institutional network of faculty, alumni, and 
former students — were credited as the most consequential mechanism in job 
acquisition. Walk-in applications and job fair attendance were also widely practiced. 
Persistent unemployment among a minority of respondents was attributed to limited 
professional networks, insufficient prior work experience, and a perceived scarcity of 
available job opportunities within their target domains. 
The BSBA curriculum was affirmed as professionally relevant by 93.6% of the 
respondents, and graduates identified specific competencies — including customer 
service, client relations, analytical problem-solving, and communication — as having 
direct application in their workplace roles. Program-aligned activities such as marketing 
campaigns, data gathering, research functions, and community extension also 
translated meaningfully into professional practice. 
The majority of employed graduates are concentrated within Angeles City and 
Pampanga, where they report high levels of workplace adaptability and express that 
their educational preparation remains a substantive asset in their current roles. 
To enhance the future competitiveness and labor market readiness of graduates, the 
following domains are recommended for curricular reinforcement: entrepreneurial 
orientation, communication efficacy, critical thinking and decision-making capacity, 
linguistic proficiency, and ICT competency. It is further recommended that the institution 
actively formalize and expand industry partnership agreements with commercial entities 
within Angeles City and Pampanga, thereby strengthening its pipeline to the primary 
labor markets absorbing its graduates.
