The Relationship between Online Selling Through Social Media and Impulse 
Purchases among Young Professionals 
Abstract 
The aim of this study is to determine if there is a connection between online selling via social media and 
impulse purchases. The steps in the methodology are as follows: Quantitative research, particularly 
descriptive research, is used to design research that seeks to describe the current state of variables. With a 
total of 387 Young Professional respondents from Angeles City, Pampanga, the researchers also gave 
self-made questionnaires and used a Likert scale as the basis for answering the survey. To examine the 
link between social commerce and impulsive purchase decisions, the data were analyzed using statistical 
analysis using mean rating and Pearson r correlation. It was found that the Young Professional's impulse 
purchase behavior and social commerce (hedonic and social) had a significant association. The 
researchers recommended that social commerce be more reliable to gain trust and credibility, that sales 
promotions be created to improve customer retention, and that good customer service is provided to give 
customers a more enjoyable experience and add value. The findings point to the need for more research 
into other factors that influence social commerce. 
Keywords: Social Commerce, Hedonic Value, Utilitarian Value, Impulse Buying Decision. 
 
Introduction and Review of Related Literature 
Social media has penetrated every area of people's lives. It becomes a tool for society, allowing people to 
connect and communicate. It should come as no surprise that, with the fast advancement of technology, 
people's shopping behaviors have changed as well. Social networking has developed into a tool for 
marketing and commerce, known as Social Commerce. The use of social networking sites like Facebook, 
Instagram, and Twitter to promote and sell products and services is known as social commerce 
(Dollarhide, 2020). 
Impulse Buying Behavior is acknowledged by marketers and academics all around the world. Customer 
shopping behavior is influenced by both the retail environment and impulsive purchases. Customers are 
usually thought to be more influenced by the main items and services on offer when making purchase 
decisions (Akram et al., 2016). Impulsive purchase behavior is a serious issue in the retail business and 
marketing, according to Saad et al., (2015). It's a habit that makes up a significant part of all things sold 
each year across a wide range of industries. 
Consumers' online purchase behavior is affected by information found in online reviews, according to 
Khem et al., (2018). According to the research, consumers' perceived hedonic value from reading internet 
reviews increases their browsing activity. Customers' tendency to buy impulsively and, as a result, their 
impulse purchasing behavior is positively influenced by browsing. Furthermore, customers with high 
impulsiveness place a greater value on the hedonic worth of online reviews, whereas consumers with low 
impulsiveness value utilitarian value more. Customers with a high level of impulsivity find that browsing 
has a stronger influence on their tendency to make impulsive purchases. 
The aim of the study is to determine if there is a connection between social commerce and impulse 
purchases. The researchers are interested in the respondents' demographic profile as well as the 

relationship between social commerce characteristics and impulse purchase decisions based on hedonic 
and utilitarian values. 
 
Methods 
Research Design 
Quantitative research, namely descriptive research, was used by the researchers. This research requires 
quantitative data collected from spontaneous purchases made by young professionals in Angeles City. To 
answer the questions and achieve the study's goals, the researchers used a survey research method to 
gather data. 
Participants of the Study 
The respondents were selected using a purposive sampling approach by the researchers. The researchers 
needed 387 people to complete the study. The views, attitudes, and opinions of respondents from Angeles 
City, specifically millennial or generation Y workers between the ages of 23 and 35, were chosen as the 
focus of the study. 
Data Gathering 
The survey questionnaire was used as the study instrument. Respondents could use the Likert scale 
method to choose their responses. Purposive sampling strategies are used to distribute surveys to 
individual respondents in Angeles City using Google Forms uploaded via Facebook Messenger. 
Procedure for Data Processing and Analysis 
The data is assessed to see if the hypotheses are valid. Statistical Analysis is used to address research 
questions by describing data using analytical methods (i.e. text, numerical). In order to respond to the 
research challenge, the researchers used a Likert scale. To examine the data, the researchers used Mean 
Rating and Pearson-R Correlation as statistical tools. The average of sample data is calculated based on 
the items of relative significance, and Pearson-R Correlation determines whether and how strongly two 
variables are related. 
Reliability Statistics: Cronbach's Alpha = .903; N of Items = 15 
 
Results and Discussion 
According to the findings, there are 127 (32.8 percent) men and 260 (67.2 percent) women. The 
demographic profile "11,691–23,381" had the highest frequency of 147 (38.0 percent), while the 
demographic profile "46,762 and above" had the lowest frequency of 55 (14.2 percent). 
"As I browse in Social Commerce, it fulfills my curiosity" received the highest mean rating of 3.36 with 
the descriptive rating of "Strongly Agree," while "As I browsed in Social Commerce, I truly felt satisfied 
and relaxed" received the lowest mean rating of 3.01 with the descriptive rating of "Agree." Overall, 
Hedonic Value has a mean of 3.18 and a descriptive rating of "Agree." 
The statement "I explore Social Commerce's (Facebook, Instagram, or Twitter) product/s or service/s 
because it is helpful" was shown to have the highest Utilitarian Value. On the other hand, the statement "I 
explore in Social Commerce because I can get more value on the stated product/s or service/s" had the

"Agree," it had the lowest mean rating of 3.06. Overall, the mean Utilitarian Value score is 3.18, with a 
descriptive score of "Agree." 
Notably, the result shows that the statement "When I browse on Social Commerce apps, I have a desire to 
buy things that are not included in my specified purchasing goal" is connected with Impulse Buying 
Decision. The statement "When I browse through Social Commerce, I sometimes buy product/s or 
service/s irresponsibly" had the highest mean rating of 2.95 with the descriptive rating of "Agree." The 
descriptive rating of "Agree" had the lowest mean rating of 2.65. Overall, the mean score for Impulse 
Buying Decision is 2.75, using "Agree" as the descriptive rating. 
The p-value of 0.001 is lower than the significance level/alpha level of 0.05 (p<0.05) in the test done to

Furthermore, there was a slight positive connection between the hedonic value of social commerce and

Based on a study that looked at the link between social commerce and impulsive purchases there is a

Conclusion 
The study examined the link between social commerce and impulse purchases among Angeles City's 
young professionals. The dependent variables: hedonic and utilitarian value, as well as the demographic 
profile: sex, and monthly income, are used to analyze and confirm the underlying factor in impulsive 
purchase behavior in this study. The study arrives at the following conclusion based on its findings: 
The male had the lowest frequency of 127 with a 32.8 percentile, while the female had the highest 
frequency of 260 with a 67.2 percentile. According to Pradhan (2016), male and female customers 
experience impulse buying differently, with female shoppers experiencing it more frequently due to their 
similarity and quick exposure to things. 
In terms of monthly income, "46,762 and above" received the lowest frequency of 55, accounting for 14.2 
percent of the total. Young Professionals with a higher salary are less likely to be impulsive buyers, 
according to the findings. The lower the frequency and proportion of the demographic description, 
according to Akram et al (2018), the greater the income. With the descriptive rating of "Agree," the 
statement "As I browsed on Social Commerce, I felt satisfied and relaxed" obtained the lowest mean 
rating of 3.01. Consumers feel uncomfortable when browsing Social Commerce, as shown in the results 

because there is no assurance that their expectations will be met, which can lead to confusion or, worse, 
dissatisfaction. 
"I explore in Social Commerce since I can get more value on the stated product/s or service/s" had the 
lowest mean rating of 3.06 and the descriptive rating of "Agree." Because the majority of sellers in social 
commerce are small business owners, the results show that the items or services sold in social commerce 
do not offer much value when compared to physical stores. According to the findings, social commerce's 
hedonic and utilitarian values have a significant relationship with impulsive purchasing decisions but are 
not as strong as that of a physical shop. When consumers come across a product online, they are less 
likely to make spontaneous or impulsive purchases. They may look at it out of curiosity, but they are less 
likely to buy it. 
 
Recommendations 
To avoid misleading customers, it is important that product or service information advertised be realistic 
or truthful to make them feel satisfied and calm. To build reliability and trust, sellers should show genuine 
product photographs rather than enhanced versions. Customers should be given discounts and encouraged 
to fill out comment coupons, which will work as reviews of their experience after they purchase the 
goods. Other potential clients will have a reference point in terms of the online seller's credibility as a 
result of this action. Because people learn about the quality of a product or service by reading evaluations 
from other customers, this may help build confidence with the target buyers. Furthermore, the researchers 
suggest offering free shipping above a certain number of things, since logistics services may add value, 
and improving product packaging to highlight the best features of the product to provide more extra value 
to the products or services offered in Social Commerce. Consumers rate items based on the uniqueness of 
their packaging, according to the survey, and are willing to try products that are nicely wrapped. 
Customers can reach the vendor directly if they have any questions or complaints about the goods, and the 
seller will respond as fast as possible. Customers develop trust and loyalty when they receive excellent 
customer service. 
Consumers should also be aware of their behavior and check customer reviews to assess the reliability of 
items or services provided in Social Commerce, as well as post feedback afterward, according to the 
study. Finally, similar research should be conducted to support these findings, and while conducting a 
survey, the number of participants should be equally distributed in terms of demographic profile: Sex, 
Age, and Gender (Male and Female). It will be easy to determine which Sex is more likely to engage in 
Social Commerce's Impulse Buying Decision using this information.
