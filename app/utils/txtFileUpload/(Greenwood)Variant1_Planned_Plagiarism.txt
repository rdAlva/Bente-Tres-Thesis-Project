Background of the Case 
Established in 1998 under the leadership of Jeff Nuss, Greenwood Resources was built 
upon an innovative fusion of varietal forestry practices and rigorous plantation 
management. The conviction driving Nuss's foundational vision was the creation of 
enduring products — a vision made actionable by his deep expertise in bio resources, 
which enabled him to identify a product category capable of yielding long-term 
competitive differentiation. At the time, sustainable forestry attracted no competing 
investment; precisely this absence of rivals emboldened his strategic entry into the 
sector. Nuss not only recognized the inherent expense and temporal demands of the 
industry but also concluded, through careful market assessment, that such barriers 
would deter most prospective investors from committing either capital or time. 
Consumer-side dynamics, as articulated by Dess et al. (2018), create demand 
conditions wherein market pressure compels organizations to innovate, refine, or 
augment their offerings. Greenwood Resources, positioned as a foremost enterprise in 
environmental tree plantation management, channeled its competencies into high-yield 
asset cultivation. For more than a decade, profitability proved elusive; however, the

Main Problem 
A multifaceted set of organizational and managerial challenges confronted Greenwood 
Resources in its pursuit of global sustainable expansion. Foremost among these was 
the structural complexity surrounding simultaneous operational commitments. The 
large-scale poplar plantation development in Oregon encountered compounding 
difficulties stemming from divergent corporate strategies and culturally conditioned 
organizational behaviors. A determination was required regarding whether concentrated 
investment in one initiative or balanced allocation across both represented the optimal 
path — a judgment incumbent upon Nuss and his senior leadership team. 

Complementing this strategic dilemma, a cluster of internal deficiencies compounded 
the firm's vulnerability: documentation of plantation territories lacked rigor; intellectual 
property protections were inadequately enforced; available investment capital was 
insufficient; exposure to socio-political instability remained considerable; cross-cultural 
operational friction persisted; and the broader economic climate posed recessionary 
risks. Shareholders and investors alike required transparent and cogent communication 
of these interdependencies to prevent misaligned expectations and misdirected 
resolution efforts.

Alternative Courses of Action 
Solution 1: Strategic Management Framework 
Advantage: The application of a strategic management framework enables coherent 
alignment across all organizational divisions, integrating divisional tactics within a 
unified corporate strategy. This structured approach equips decision-makers with the 
analytical clarity needed to evaluate whether investment in one or both plantation 
initiatives is warranted. 
Disadvantage: An overly restrictive prioritization may foreclose the opportunity to 
expand operations and attract the capital interest of external investors — particularly 
with respect to the Oregon poplar plantation. 
Solution 2: Legal Compliance and Documentation 
Advantage: Rigorous adherence to jurisdictional land-use laws and forestry resource 
regulations ensures that operations are both legally sound and formally substantiated. 
Disadvantage: The enforcement of compliance mandates may generate internal 
administrative burdens, potentially introducing operational disruption and managerial 
friction within the organization. 
Solution 3: Capital Investment Development 
Advantage: Securing diversified funding sources ensures financial continuity and 
mitigates the risk of resource shortfalls; moreover, it facilitates a comparative 
assessment of investment vehicles in terms of expected yield. 

Disadvantage: The organization's internal operating cash flows may prove insufficient to

Recommendation 
Upon synthesis of the preceding analysis, the optimal course of action is determined to 
be the concurrent adoption of Alternative Solutions 1 and 3 — strategic management 
and capital investment development. The rationale is twofold: deliberate, high-caliber 
decision-making at the executive and senior management levels is essential to 
sustaining long-term organizational viability; and the procurement of adequate 
investment capital is indispensable to maintaining uninterrupted operational capacity. 
Together, these two interventions address both the governance and financial 
dimensions of Greenwood's challenges. 
Mission 
Central to Greenwood Conservancy's institutional purpose is the establishment of a 
protected natural refuge dedicated to the stewardship and enhancement of existing 
ecological habitat. This mission is operationalized through the deliberate preservation of 
diverse environmental zones — including open fields, woodlands, and marshland 
systems. 
The refuge functions as a guardian of indigenous wildlife communities, encompassing 
waterfowl, songbirds, birds of prey, and native plant species including wildflowers and 
trees. Beyond ecological protection, the refuge serves as a permanent barrier against 
developmental encroachment over a substantial land area, while simultaneously 
fulfilling a role as an educational, aesthetic, and environmental resource for surrounding 
communities.
