Examining How Experiential Branding Shapes Purchasing Behavior Among Shoppers at Baby 
Charm, Marquee Mall 
 
Abstract 
Centered on Baby Charm’s Marquee Mall outlet, this investigation explored the extent to 
which experiential brand engagement shapes consumers’ inclination to make purchases. 
Four experiential dimensions — Sensory, Affective, Behavioral, and Intellectual — were 
operationalized as the primary analytical constructs through which this relationship was 
assessed. A researcher-developed survey instrument was administered to a sample of 292 
participants, and the collected data were evaluated through mean scoring procedures 
alongside the Pearson R Correlation statistical framework. Across all four dimensions, 
respondents’ scores fell within the “Strongly Agree” classification. These outcomes led the 
investigators to conclude that a strong, positive association exists between experiential 
brand engagement and consumers’ intention to purchase. It is therefore recommended that 
Baby Charm prioritize the enhancement of its brand experience delivery, given that 
measurable correlations between its experiential branding components and consumer 
purchasing inclination were empirically established. 
(Keywords: Brand Experience, Sensory, Affective, Behavioral, Intellectual, Consumer Purchase 
Intention) 
 
Introduction and Review of Related Literature 
Defined by Rouse (2018) as a mode of experiential marketing, brand engagement refers to 
the deliberate construction of an all-encompassing set of conditions by an organization, 
with the intention of shaping how consumers emotionally perceive a particular product or 
corporate identity. Through engagement across multiple touchpoints, organizations strive 
to cultivate an atmosphere of mutual trust, goodwill, and constancy — forging a meaningful 
link between the brand and a specific human need or emotional state. Schmidt (2019) 
similarly characterized brand engagement as a promotional discipline that assembles a 
comprehensive array of circumstances deliberately designed to elicit affirmative emotions 
and favorable sentiments among consumers. How a consumer evaluates and emotionally 
responds to a product or enterprise is significantly shaped by brand experience, which 
further functions to cultivate brand awareness and nurture long-term brand loyalty. A 
frequently cited illustration of successful brand engagement is the deliberate embedding of 
a brand into a defining personal milestone within its target market’s lifecycle. 
Consumer loyalty is among the key organizational outcomes made more attainable through 
robust brand experience. Feedback loops between businesses and their customers are also 
established as a result. According to Ramayah (2018), varied forms of brand experience 

were found to exert differential influence on purchase intention, with brand experience 
being recognized as instrumental in fostering purchasing intent and sustaining long-term 
business viability. Furthermore, this construct has been positioned as one of the most 
intellectually fertile areas of inquiry within consumer research over the preceding ten 
years. Nevertheless, in contrast to other brand-associated frameworks, its examination 
within the academic literature has more often been implicit rather than explicit, as Solerio 
(2019) observed. 
Cleff (2014) identified four experiential dimensions — sensory, affective, behavioral, and 
intellectual — all of which were found to exert a favorable influence on consumers’ 
intention to purchase. 
Consumer purchase intention denotes a buyer’s willingness or predisposition to acquire a 
specific product or service. It operates as a dependent variable that responds to a variety of 
internal and external influences. As Tang (2015) articulated, the concept of brand 
experience was configured holistically — integrating sensory, affective, behavioral, and 
intellectual dimensions — with consumer purchasing intention as the dependent outcome 
variable. 
Parental purchasing behavior, as Latha (2016) noted, is meaningfully tied to infant welfare 
concerns, with parents consistently seeking the highest quality goods available in the 
marketplace to ensure the health and safety of their children. Ebrahim (2016) further 
observed that such consumers actively gravitate toward brands that engage them across 
sensorial, emotional, and creative dimensions. Additionally, research by Aydin (2014) 
suggests that trust exerts a stronger effect on repurchase intention than commitment does 
in the context of brand relationships. Sen (2017) found that brand experiences cultivated 
within organized retail environments consistently generate customer satisfaction, thereby 
influencing purchasing decisions during the shopping process. 
Sensory 
How frontline employees engage with customers constitutes a determining factor in 
purchase intention, as argued by Iglesias (2019). More broadly, the sensory dimension of 
brand experience has been identified as a significant driver of consumer purchasing 
inclination toward specific brands, as Moreira (2017) demonstrated. The implication is that 
sensory engagement functions as one of the most potent mechanisms for elevating 
consumer purchase intent levels. By forging deeper emotional ties with consumers through 
the five human senses, brands are able to cultivate preference and attachment (Peter, 
2018). To leverage this dynamic, managers are encouraged to adopt individualized 
relational strategies — specifically consumer-brand relationship frameworks — that 
generate memorable brand encounters and thereby stimulate repeat patronage (Xie, 2019). 
Ndaaba (2016) further asserts that within brand engagement frameworks, sensory 
measurement encompasses both the physical and affective impressions a brand makes on 
the consumer, engaging all five perceptual modalities: hearing, sight, smell, taste, and touch. 

Affective 
Emotional stimulation — often described under the “feel” dimension — pertains to the 
activation of a consumer’s sensations and feelings, whereas “think” engages 
problem-solving cognitive processes. Affective variables including satisfaction and 
loyalty-driven commitment bear a direct relationship to purchasing intention insofar as 
they address and fulfill consumer needs and desires, potentially culminating in a purchase 
decision. The affective dimension, therefore, occupies a critically significant position in 
shaping outcomes (Bang, 2015). 
Wagner (2017) established that the affective dimension is associated with the cultivation of 
positive emotional states and with elevated rates of spontaneous purchasing and store 
allegiance. Qader (2017) provided substantiating evidence that emotional attachment to a 
brand — arising from satisfaction with the brand’s performance and fulfillment of its 
promises — positively predicts purchasing intent, with continued patronage as the 
downstream outcome. Emotional intentions, once identified and made visible, can also 
serve as practical input for manufacturers seeking to refine their product and service 
offerings. Moreover, as Tsui (2018) noted, affective information is highly applicable in the 
context of personalized product recommendations. Ultimately, as Quero (2015) concluded, 
affective commitment constitutes a meaningful predictor of consumer purchasing 
intentions. 
Behavioral 
The behavioral dimension, as Kompella (2019) argued, may be understood as the automatic 
identification of human emotional states and feelings as expressed through habitual 
purchasing patterns associated with a given brand. Fahim (2019) contributed to this 
discussion by noting that a consumer’s social identity — which helps explain inter-group 
behavioral variation — provides a framework of norms governing group-consistent 
conduct. Stereotypes, which represent a pivotal feature of social identity, encode physical, 
mental, and psychological characteristics attributed to typical members of a given group. 
Once these attributed characteristics are applied to define a group, they exert a shaping 
influence on how individual group members behave. While often somewhat exaggerated, 
stereotypes function predominantly by amplifying perceived differences between social 
categories. A negative stereotype, for example, may generate distorted self-perceptions that 
lead individuals to underperform relative to their actual capabilities (Zelinsky, 2019). 
The social identity framework, by defining norms for group behavior, thereby offers a 
framework for explaining behavioral differences across social collectives. A defining feature 
of one’s social identity is its associated generalizations: the physical, psychological, and 
cognitive attributes assigned to a representative member of a particular group. Attributing 
a set of characteristics to a social group shapes the behavioral tendencies of individuals 
who affiliate with it. As such, the exaggeration inherent in many stereotypes intensifies 
perceptions of intergroup difference. For populations that face systemic discrimination, the 
effects of stereotype threat are especially pronounced (Vecci, 2019). Wang (2015) further 
established that trust, operating as a mediating variable, influences how purchasing 
intention is shaped through the behavioral dimension. 

Intellectual 
Li (2018) characterized the intellectual dimension as being fundamentally concerned with 
cognitive engagement — encompassing contemplation, curiosity arousal, analytical 
reasoning, and creative ideation. Complementing this, Ahn (2018) found that both 
experiential and intellectually elaborated forms of brand encounter are activated 
concurrently during the process by which brand recognition quality and purchasing 
intention are formed. 
Willems (2019) proposed that the intellectual dimension may offer particular utility for 
emerging managers who aim to cultivate positive consumer attitudes and generate 
purchasing intent directed at their brands, as well as for those endeavoring to animate their 
brands through innovative engagement. Pandowo (2016) similarly characterized the 
intellectual dimension as engaging the consumer’s imaginative reasoning and their capacity 
to perceive familiar things from novel angles. 
Roy (2017) established that intellectual capital is a decisive determinant of a company’s 
operational success and long-term sustainability. Brand experience, at the same time, 
functions as a test of consumer loyalty within any organizational context. Baby Charm is a 
retail merchandising enterprise: finished goods are procured and subsequently resold 
directly to consumers. As a comprehensive one-stop destination for infant needs, the store 
supplies mothers with an extensive range of products spanning layette, bed linens, feeding 
supplies, infant apparel, footwear, health and grooming items, baby safety products, 
furniture, and toys. Premium product quality, competitive value for money, broad 
merchandise selection, and shopping convenience — supported by guidance from a 
professionally trained sales team — are among the core value propositions offered. In 
summary, the store is positioned around complete solutions, best-value pricing, and 
high-quality goods and services. Three locations have been established by Baby Charm 
across the country. 
 
Conceptual Framework 
This investigation maps the relationship between Brand Experience — as operationalized 
through four dimensions: tangible, emotional, behavioral, and cognitive — and Consumer 
Purchase Intention at Baby Charm. The study’s results are intended to determine whether 
Brand Experience bears a statistically significant relationship to Purchase Intention.

Scope and Limitations 
This study confines its examination to the relationship between brand experience and 
consumer purchase intention, with the scope restricted to Baby Charm’s Marquee Mall 
location. The central objective is to assess the degree to which brand experience aligns with 
consumer needs. The study’s participant pool comprised 292 mothers who shop at Baby 
Charm. 
 
Methods 
Research Design 
A descriptive research approach was employed to systematically characterize the 
information necessary to understand how four dimensions — sensory, affective, 
intellectual, and behavioral — relate to consumer purchase intention. Structured in survey 
format, the descriptive methodology employed data collection instruments calibrated to 
capture experiential responses within a predetermined classification system. Quantitative 
approaches prioritize objective measurement and statistical, scientific, or numerical 
analysis of data obtained through surveys and studies, or through computational 
manipulation of pre-existing quantifiable data. Correlational research, classified as a form 
of non-experimental inquiry, involves the measurement of two variables to assess the 
statistical relationship between them without the influence of extraneous variables. Survey 
findings from respondents were subsequently compiled, contrasted, and generalized. 
Consistency, precision, and reliability in survey-derived data were key priorities for the 
research team. 

Participants of the Study 
Mothers represent a demographically significant consumer segment given that 
unconditional parental love forms the basis of trust and emotional security in children’s 
developmental lives (Savage, 2007), and because mothers constitute a high-demand market 
for furniture, toys, and infant supplies (Lester, 2008). 
The study’s participant pool consisted of 292 mothers who actively shop at Baby Charm, 
Marquee Mall. The Raosoft Sample Size Calculator was used to derive the required sample 
from a population base. The total population of mothers in Angeles City, Pampanga — as 
documented by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) — stands at 83,603. The Raosoft 
tool, functioning as a computational program that generates sample size estimates for 
research and survey purposes, simultaneously provides confidence interval calculations 
and margin-of-error minimization functionality. Its automated computation yielded a 
required sample of 292, corresponding to the number of questionnaires ultimately 
distributed. 
Research Instrument 
A self-developed survey questionnaire served as the primary data collection instrument. Its 
function was to measure the impact of brand experience dimensions — sensory, affective, 
behavioral, and intellectual — on consumer purchase intention. Responses were captured 
using a four-point Likert scale: Strongly Disagree (1), Disagree (2), Agree (3), and Strongly 
Agree (4). 
Data Gathering Procedures 
A straightforward survey questionnaire, designed for ease of comprehension, was 
developed and administered to participants. Questions were formulated to ensure they 
meaningfully captured the dimensions of the topic under investigation. Questionnaire 
distribution and data processing required a considerable investment of time and effort. A 
pilot testing phase lasting one week was conducted prior to the main survey 
administration, with the purpose of verifying the instrument’s reliability. 
Data Processing and Analysis Procedure 
The Likert scale range was defined as follows: Strongly Disagree (1), Disagree (2), Agree 
(3), and Strongly Agree (4). To determine the level of each factor and assess whether brand 
experience is associated with consumer purchase intention, Mean Rating and Correlation 
were applied as the primary statistical procedures. Mean Rating reflects the average score 
of tallied survey responses and serves as the basis for evaluating how brand experience 
affects Customer Purchase Intention.

Result and Discussion 
With respect to the Sensory dimension, the survey item receiving the lowest average score 
(3.59, “Strongly Agree”) was the statement pertaining to positive consumer feedback about 
Baby Charm. The highest-rated item (3.69, “Strongly Agree”) related to the store’s breadth 
of product offerings. The composite mean for Sensory was 3.6315, rated as “Strongly 
Agree.” 
Regarding Affective, product ease of use received the highest mean rating of 3.76 (“Strongly 
Agree”), while the item concerning the store’s attentiveness to customer care registered the 
lowest at 3.53 (“Strongly Agree”). The overall mean for the Affective dimension was 3.6267, 
interpreted as “Strongly Agree.” 
For the Behavioral dimension, product safety for infants ranked highest with a mean of 
3.70, while the item expressing that Baby Charm consistently meets expectations received 
the lowest score of 3.50. Both ratings — along with the composite mean of 3.5952 — were 
interpreted as “Strongly Agree.” 
Concerning the Intellectual dimension, affordable pricing relative to competitors earned the 
highest mean rating of 3.62, while compliance with international standards received the 
lowest at 3.45. The overall mean for this dimension was 3.5596, also interpreted as 
“Strongly Agree.” 
With respect to Purchase Intention, the item concerning trusted brand status was rated 
highest at 3.60 (“Strongly Agree”), and the item regarding regular product and promotional 
updates received the lowest score of 3.25. The composite Purchase Intention mean was 
3.3719, rated as “Strongly Agree.” 
Hypothesis testing was conducted using the Pearson Product Moment R Correlation to 
evaluate the associations between each Brand Experience dimension and Consumer 

Purchase Intention. All four null hypotheses were rejected at a significance level of 0.000 
(p-value < 0.05). The Sensory dimension demonstrated a strong positive correlation (r = 
.600); Affective similarly yielded a strong positive correlation (r = .623); Behavioral 
produced the strongest correlation among the four dimensions (r = .708); and Intellectual 
was also characterized as a strong positive correlation (r = .684). 
 
Conclusion 
The purpose of this investigation was to assess the influence of experiential branding on 
consumer purchase intention. The following conclusions were drawn for each dimension 
under Brand Experience: 
The Sensory dimension recorded a mean of 3.6315 (“Strongly Agree”), with item scores 
ranging from 3.59 (lowest: positive consumer feedback) to 3.69 (highest: wide product 
selection). 
The Affective dimension yielded a mean of 3.6267 (“Strongly Agree”), with scores ranging 
from 3.53 (lowest: customer care quality) to 3.76 (highest: ease of product use). 
The Behavioral dimension produced a mean of 3.5952 (“Strongly Agree”), with scores 
ranging from 3.50 (lowest: consistent non-disappointment) to 3.70 (highest: infant product 
safety). 
The Intellectual dimension achieved a mean of 3.5596 (“Strongly Agree”), with scores 
ranging from 3.45 (lowest: compliance with international standards) to 3.62 (highest: 
competitive pricing). 
The null hypotheses were uniformly rejected. Accordingly, it is concluded that a statistically 
significant relationship exists between Brand Experience and Consumer Purchase 
Intention. 
 
Recommendation 
As a well-established brand that offers affordable infant products, Baby Charm is 
encouraged to consider the following improvements: 
For Sensory: Active monitoring of consumer feedback and reviews is essential. The 
deployment of a robust Social Media presence is recommended to enable ongoing 
communication with customers, facilitate prompt responsiveness to feedback and inquiries, 
and reinforce consumer trust. 
For Affective: Hiring personnel whose personal values and interpersonal style align with 
Baby Charm’s organizational culture is strongly advisable. Employees serve as the 
company’s most direct interface with consumers, making them central to the fulfillment of 
customer needs. 

For Behavioral: Sustained attention to product quality assurance and ongoing monitoring 
of consumer behavioral trends within the market are necessary. Remaining current with 
market developments is of strategic importance in the contemporary retail environment. 
For Intellectual: Regular introduction of new product lines should be considered, 
alongside rigorous assurance that all products comply with applicable licensing and safety 
regulatory standards. 
 
Definition of Terms 
Brand — a product category associated with a specific manufacturer under a designated 
commercial name; also the recognizable identity by which consumers identify a product. 
Experience — firsthand contact with and observation of actual facts or occurrences. 
Sensory — pertaining to the body’s perceptual systems and the transmission or reception 
of stimuli through the five senses. 
Affective — characterizing phenomena that are emotionally influenced, emotionally 
produced, or emotionally expressive. 
Intellectual — relating to a person’s cognitive capacity to engage in reasoning, analysis, 
and the comprehension of ideas and information. 
Behavioural — an action that is directly observable and quantifiably measurable. 
Purchase intention — the consumer’s predisposition or inclination to acquire a product 
or service; the likelihood of a purchase decision following evaluation.
