Activity 7: Understanding Life through the Lens of Religion and Science: A REFLECTION 
 
 
The fact that science and religion have different worldviews is frequently brought up by 
those who claim that they are incompatible. Science is based on observation and data from 
experience, whereas religion is based on faith and revelation. As an example, a lot of religious 
people think that God created the universe, but scientists think that natural selection played a role 
in the universe's creation. Religious beliefs have driven the search for a natural explanation of the 
universe. For example, the desire to fully understand God's creation has motivated many scientists. 
Science is interested in comprehending the natural world; religion is concerned with issues of 
morality, meaning, and purpose. There are occasions when science and religion disagree, but there 
are other situations in which they can get along and even learn from one another. In the end, how 
well both fields of study interact will depend on how eager people are to have professional, open 
discussions with one another. Future developments in the relationship between religion and science 
are probably in store. There will inevitably be new challenges to religious beliefs as scientific 
knowledge expands. It's also achievable, though, that religion and science will discover new means 
of getting along or even working together. In the end, decisions made by people and communities 
will determine how religion and science interact in the future.
