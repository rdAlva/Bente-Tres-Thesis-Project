1. Discuss the 3 pillars of Information Assurance in relation to safeguarding information 
or data  
 
         Confidentiality - It guarantees that only authorized people can access information.    
This includes protecting the information from illegal access, disclosure, or theft. It is 
essential for protecting delicate information, such as private information, financial 
information, or confidential data.  
 
          Integrity – assures the truthfulness, precision, and integrity of the information. In 
other words, data must be safeguarded against illegal change, deletion, or corruption. It is 
crucial for preserving the accuracy and credibility of information. 
 
          Availability - guarantees that data is available and useable when required. This entails 
safeguarding data against interruptions, outages, and denial-of-service assaults. It is crucial 
for making sure that authorized users get prompt access to the data they require. 
 
2. Briefly explain the attack methods on how these 3 pillars can be compromised. 
 
 
Hacking and Data leaks compromises Confidentiality, these individuals gain access 
to sensitive data by exploiting system vulnerabilities or using stolen credentials. 
Confidential information is accidentally or intentionally released to unauthorized parties.  
 
Malware Infections and Unauthorized Changes compromises Integrity, attackers 
modify, delete, or corrupt data, compromising its accuracy and reliability and Unauthorized 
individuals make unauthorized modifications to data, compromising its integrity. 
 
Ransomware Attacks and Hardware Failures compromises Availability, attackers 
encrypt data and demand payment for decryption, rendering information inaccessible and 
Hardware malfunctions or outages can disrupt access to information systems and data. 
 

3. Explain how Authentication Authorization and Accounting contribute to Information 
assurance 
 
 
By implementing access control, tracking user behavior, and upholding 
responsibility, the crucial architecture of authentication, authorization, and accounting 
(AAA) greatly helps to information assurance (IA).  
Authentication, it confirms the user identities and limiting access to those who 
have been given permission. This procedure promotes availability by ensuring that 
authorized users may access resources without being hindered, protects integrity by 
lowering the danger of data tampering, and enhances confidentiality by preventing 
unauthorized access to sensitive data. 
 For Authorization, it limits the users to only the information they are authorized 
to view or alter by specifying access privileges. This approach ensures availability by 
making sure resources are distributed properly, upholds integrity by preventing 
unauthorized actions, and enforces confidentiality by restricting access to sensitive data.  
For Accounting, it promotes availability by proactively identifying potential 
performance issues that could influence system uptime, ensures integrity by detecting 
unauthorized changes, and enhances confidentiality by enabling the detection of 
unwanted access attempts. 
 Organizations may successfully safeguard their information assets, uphold the 
confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data, and improve overall information 
assurance by employing AAA techniques.
