Poverty Leads to Violence 
The truth is poverty is not just a lack of money—it has many complicated dimensions that result 
in both physical and emotional problems. The physical effects include malnutrition, poor health, 
lack of housing, and other poverty-related conditions. The emotional effects are felt within the 
individual's family, friends, and community. 
But before I go into these dimensions of poverty, let me first share some types of poverty that 
lead to violence. I will call out specific examples where they are most common, but first I want 
to provide an overview for those who are unfamiliar with the concept. 
Poverty often has many dimensions. Here are four I use to define it—unemployment, single 
parenthood, lack of education, and drug/alcohol abuse/addiction. Poverty can be caused by any 
or all these factors at different times in a person's life. But when a person is living in poverty for 
a long period, the physical and emotional effects lead to violence. 
 
Unemployment. The percentage of people unemployed is usually 2–7 times higher among 
those living below the poverty line than those who are not poor. Even those who have had jobs 
but can't find them can also become violent as they deal with the economic pressures on 
themselves, and their families caused by unemployment. They become desperate and will do 
anything to feed their families, including crime and violence. For example, in some cities, 
unemployed people will steal to feed their families. When they get caught, the police will arrest 
them, and they will be sent to jail, where they will be treated as criminals. 
 Single parenthood. Children from single-parent homes have a higher rate of poverty and 
violence than those from two-parent homes; they are also more likely to live in low-income 
neighborhoods where the chance of encountering gangs is greater. 
 
Lack of education. It is no secret that poverty is the greatest foe of education at all levels. 
Students from low-income families are more likely to suffer from health problems. The 
youngsters' capacity to study and learn is affected by their poor existence. Getting the correct 
education can not only raise individuals out of poverty, but it can also improve their health, 
solve their financial problems, and alter their life. 
Drug/alcohol abuse/addiction. The link between addiction and violence is well documented. In 
many ways, drug use is a symptom of poverty; drugs are often used to temporarily relieve the 
pain of unemployment or to fill the void of loneliness or broken relationships. In addition, many 
people addicted to alcohol and other drugs are often unemployed and homeless. The cycle of 
poverty, violence, and addiction is a vicious cycle. It is not difficult to see the four kinds of links 

between poverty and violence that I have described. Research shows that poverty causes all 
these

Why poverty leads to violence is important to society as a whole To reduce the prevalence of 
violence, we should identify the key factors and target the main reasons accordingly. The direct 
factors leading to poverty and violence are lack of opportunity, lack of education, and lack of a 
decent standard of living. Poverty has a direct impact on all of these. As poverty and inequality 
become more entrenched, people are increasingly eager to solve the problem. To obtain 
resources for survival, we may become more willing to commit crimes. Because of this, we are 
also more likely to have violent conflicts with others. In other words, when we face more social 
and economic challenges, we are more likely to become violent.
