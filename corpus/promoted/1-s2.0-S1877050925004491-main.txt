Exploratory customer discovery on Veblen goods using ChatGPT 
and agent-based modeling system for business simulation
Zachary Matthew Alabastroa,*, Reuel Matthew Cordovaa, Joseph Benjamin Ilagana, 
Jose Ramon Ilagana
aBusiness Insights Laboratory for Development, Ateneo de Manila University, Loyola Heights, Quezon City 1108, Philippines
Abstract 
The study of demand in a dynamic and complex market economy has been a point of discussion in observing consumer behavior. 
Entrepreneurs have struggled with market research as customer discovery can be costly and time-consuming. The concept behind 
non-intuitive demand curves has increased the complexities of predicting demand. Large language models (LLMs) are observed 
to be computational models for humans, having the innate capacity to simulate human behavior and decision-making. Through a 
simulation model and the agent-based modeling system (ABMS) approach, entrepreneurs can utilize LLMs by using their ability 
for customer discovery. By applying prompting frameworks and chain-of-thought (CoT), LLMs such as ChatGPT-3.5 can generate 
customer willingness-to-pay (WTP) without fine-tuning the model. The study experimented on two business models that pertained 
to Veblen demand and prompted GPT-3.5 to simulate WTP without any explicit references to “Veblen” to determine its inherent 
knowledge. The results show that some customer simulations generated with GPT-3.5 appear to form a non-inverted demand curve. 
Future studies may be considered to understand the LLM’s capability of perceiving socio-economic and psycho-emotional behavior 
regarding product demand and consumption.

1. Introduction
Entrepreneurs who intend to enter an industry may seek relevant information on possible customer demand for their 
products to strategize their decisions in unfamiliar conditions. However, traditional customer discovery methods such 
as interviews, surveys, and focus group discussions (FGDs) are costly [10, 18-20]. The entrepreneur is then left with 
limited options to create decisions in uncertain conditions [17]. Simulations have been proposed as an alternative to 
traditional customer discovery [14], offering low-cost alternatives by operating with a given set of parameters [21]. 
Exploratory research has been done on using the innate knowledge of large language models (LLMs) as the engine 
behind simulations [14-15]. The agent-based modeling system (ABMS) handles interactions and relationships between 
individual and autonomous agents [23, 34] and their corresponding entrepreneurial and market contexts. In essence, 
the user provides the attributes given to these agents, sets the environment of the simulation, and allows the elements 
to interact in a system with a given situation [33]. Along with ABMS, the generated human subjects were experimented 
on according to different contexts [14, 23]. With the concept of homo silicus, LLMs are designed to serve as 
computational models of humans and are trained to possess latent social information [25]. By utilizing the capabilities 
of LLMs, entrepreneurs can determine if the generated behavior from the provided business concepts would reflect 
the phenomenon of Veblen as well.
The law of demand [1] serves as a focal point of discussion in scholarly discussion. It is composed of how people 
view market prices given their scarce resources [2]. Economists typically model demand for a product as inversely 
proportional to its price [1, 3]. However, some events show that certain industries can defy the theoretical rule [4]. 
This “law of demand” is common but not universal. Certain types of goods display non-intuitive demand curves [9]. 
This paper focuses specifically on “Veblen goods,” products for which the demand is directly proportional to its price 
[5-6]. This characteristic is theorized to be caused by these goods being bought for status rather than for pure utility 
[7-8]. Automobiles, luxury wines, and high-end watches are examples of Veblen goods [11-13]. Customer willingness-
to-pay (WTP), in this context, is derived from the idea behind conspicuous consumption as a driver of luxurious appeal 
[13].
The study explores ChatGPT as a simulation tool for customer discovery, in the case of non-intuitive demand. By 
inciting its self-learning capabilities, the LLM will be tested on its ability to learn from its outputs and reflections. 
This can be observed by allowing it to generate customer personas through a set of provided attributes, reflect on the 
business models, and simulate customer behavior and feedback.
RQ1) What general approach may be taken to determine whether the LLM is inherently knowledgeable of Veblen 
products and demand, and if it can simulate customer willingness-to-pay (WTP) on certain business models?
RQ2) What are the notable considerations involved in using LLMs to simulate WTP in the context of Veblen or 
non-intuitive demand curves?
Note that the study does not assert that the simulations done through ChatGPT be considered replacements for real-
world customer discovery. This acts as a mere proposal to see if the LLM can serve as an accurate co-pilot to business 
founders on whether or not it understands such business models and consumer behavior. The first research discussion 
point aims to identify ChatGPT’s capability of inherently identifying the category of business models, in this case, 
Veblen goods. This can determine whether its data training on human behavior suffices for non-intuitive industries. It 
seeks to understand if the LLM, provided with “some help,” or conservative prompt engineering, can generate outputs 
with better accuracy.
2. Review of related literature
Economic theory suggests that the law of demand follows a complex and dynamic system of relationships [31]. In 
the case of normal consumption, individuals prefer to purchase cheaper alternatives in a perfectly competitive market 
[32]. As market demand can dictate the value of products, businesses experience the need to strengthen their pricing 
decisions so as not to be ruled out by an elastic market [2]. In identifying how the economy functions in relation to 
the circulation of value and money, economic theory explains such phenomena using statistical analysis, logical 
reasoning, and models [31]. In [22, 24], demand for normal goods was observed to increase as the real income of the 
consumer increased. Previously discussed in the theory behind Veblen, this correlates to the higher standard of living 
from the prestige that product consumption brings to the consumer [12, 26]. Exploring how Veblen demand occurs

consists of identifying specific industries that resemble the factors behind such movement. In the case of non-intuitive 
demand curves, consumer products such as luxury accessories, clothes, and automobiles [12-13] exploit the Veblen 
effect and exhibit conspicuous consumption. A research study in Russia [12] observed the growing trend behind car 
loans as a result of higher income status. It presents how people have become more inclined to incur liabilities to 
purchase “prestigious” vehicles, connecting to conspicuous consumption. In [26-27], consumers possess the urge to 
keep in proximity with high-end brands to maintain their social status. Hence, the WTP for luxurious items generally 
increases as the price increases, although extending beyond ceteris paribus, the inclusion of socio-economic status 
and household income produce complexities with human behavior [11, 13].
In the context of performing customer discovery through ABMS approach, prompting is a required task [14, 16, 
25]. No-shot prompting requires the LLM to generate output based solely on the task description, refraining from 
endowing it with any examples. No-shot prompting is dependent on specific situations, and requires the prompt to be 
explicit, concrete, and complete on its request in order to avoid potential hallucinations [28-29]. Another recent study 
[14] revealed that the zero-shot learning approach serves as a useful tool for entrepreneurs to determine the potential 
WTP of consumers targeted by their businesses. Through the implementation of chain-of-thought (CoT) techniques, 
the entrepreneur does not have to fine-tune the LLM, therefore avoiding larger costs in doing customer discovery [35]. 
By providing the agents [23] with certain preferences, biases, economic, and social information, their behavior can be
explored through simulations. A study [25] conducted using a hiring scenario illustrated the ability of the LLM to 
replicate employee responses to wage or compensation rates. The user can utilize homo silicus to predict social 
interactions, but only to a certain extent [30]. Although the model is pre-trained on large amounts of data, it cannot 
immediately understand the underlying conditions and factors that human subjects perceive. In [14], a CoT prompting 
framework was presented, generating customer WTP to a tutoring service business which resembles the general law 
of demand in the context provided. However, this does not conclude that the LLM knows about the law of demand. If 
the responses retrieved contain a certain level of similarity with real human responses, they can serve as cost-efficient 
ways of reaching out to customers [14]. Though ChatGPT may simulate customer discovery and market validation 
activities, its accuracy must further be analyzed.
3. Methodology
As done in [14], the study centers on simulating customer WTP to the provided business model and price points. 
By giving ChatGPT a persona as a business analyst, it can generate a price sensitivity analysis, resembling customer 
discovery. As an alternative to surveys and interviews, ChatGPT’s generative capability can decrease the duration of 
retrieving information on customer behavior. In this study, the simulations will be done using OpenAI’s GPT-3.5 to 
determine its inherent knowledge using its base and static information.
The study conducted two types of prompting methods. The simulation utilized zero-shot inference, both with and 
without CoT. The prompts were applied on high-end automobile and luxury handbag businesses, both of which have 
illustrated conspicuous consumption in real-world markets. Getting the WTP corresponding to a certain price range 
can help the user compute the most optimal price point for revenue generation. Experimenting on Veblen can assist 
the entrepreneur in analyzing which price point can generate the most revenue whilst retaining the overall demand.
An initial zero-shot approach, without CoT, was employed wherein all the information (the business models and 
end-goal) were placed into a single prompt. Another method consisted of CoT, where the prompts were divided into 
a series of steps for the LLM to effectively process and respond to each.

begin the generation, the LLM was prompted to generate a randomized set of attributes for each persona with certain 
parameters. The LLM was prompted to generate a list of attributes associated with customer’s spending behaviors, 
with various options provided for each attribute. Then, the LLM was tasked of randomly selecting an option for each 
of the attributes in this list whilst taking into considering the attribute dependencies and connections: (1) occupation, 
(2) age, (3) gender, (4) education level, (5) preferred shopping method, (5) interests, (6) social media usage, (7) 
residential area, (8) travel frequency, (9) social event participation, and (9) attitude towards sustainability.

modifications, ChatGPT was able to include potentially novel ideas into the model, though its value is another

Various adjustments took place in order to maintain a “clean” prompting framework. This means that nowhere in 
the prompt should any implications of Veblen occur. With regards to character attributes, the researchers removed 
any total income or monetary attributes. By removing these, the LLM refrains from using income as a signal of 
exclusivity, which could hinder the study from determining its inherent abilities. Interest in investments was also 
removed to avoid connections with conspicuous consumption and collectibles. The business models were revised to 
ensure that no explicit references or mentions of “luxury” were retained. As the goal was to see if GPT-3.5 could 
simulate the WTP of a business that resembles Veblen, this word was omitted to produce more accuracy. Both models 
had to be realistic, hence the characteristics were based on the perspective of a new business with limited supply due 
to manufacturing constraints, amateur product descriptions, and new operating procedures. Finally, the occupation 
choices, brand consciousness, and customer interests were revised so as not to imply the Veblen WTP. By keeping 
the nature of the generated human subjects more organic, the LLM can replicate real-world simulations with fairness. 
This can help determine ChatGPT’s viability as a cost-efficient alternative for customer discovery.
5. Discussion
Revisiting the research questions presented above, together with the developed context from the simulation, yield 
the following results:
RQ1) What general approach may be taken to determine whether the LLM is inherently knowledgeable of Veblen 
products and demand, and if it can simulate customer willingness-to-pay (WTP) on certain business models?
Using GPT-3.5, zero-shot inference and the CoT technique can serve as viable simulation strategies for economic 
theory and WTP. However, the information presented must be realistic and clear to prevent hallucinations. It is 
essential to propose a business model that is reasonable and realistic, along with a range of price points that resemble 
real-world products. The generated agents should also resemble organic human subjects, rather than possess attributes 
that can imply Veblen demand. By applying various prompt patterns, including persona, context manager, reflective, 
recipe, and format, simulations can aid in decreasing the time it takes to perform customer discovery. GPT-3.5 contains 
stock knowledge of pre-trained data on economic movements, helping it generate demand that resembles non-intuitive 
demand curves. This does not mean that it fully understands Veblen products or non-intuitive demand, as noted in 
economic theory. The user must be careful in prompting as not to “beg” the simulation of Veblen curves, or else the 
results may be inaccurate. By integrating ABMS into manual market research, this can assist in developing a stronger 
foundation for future entrepreneurs entering competitive industries.
RQ2) What are the notable considerations involved in using LLMs to simulate WTP in the context of Veblen or 
non-intuitive demand curves?
The problem consists of non-intuitive demand curves, hence the traditional law of demand does not apply in the 
simulations. This produces more intricacies with regards to the perceived behavior of the generated human subjects, 
especially since psychological and economic factors play an important role in conspicuous consumption. The data 
quality must be analyzed. It means that the generated personas should be clear, and that the randomized attributes 
should be consistent with each other. For example, it sounds more reasonable for a young influencer to have a higher 
social media usage than others. Moreover, the LLM is still constrained by hallucinations, hence its interpretation of

the business model may be faulty at times. This is why some trials simulated normal demand curves rather than 
Veblen. In a more subtle note, the order of prompts must be studied since changing such order may alter the way the 
LLM perceives the problem. It may see the business model and personas in a broader sense, until a business analyst 
persona is given to it. This might change the WTP curve as well. Ethical considerations must also be discussed, as 
with further iterations. Given the possible use of private information in the simulations, it is imperative to implement 
proper safeguards that protect user data. If in the case that the prompts produced optimal accuracy of simulating 
customer discovery, the LLM should not hinder people from obtaining quality jobs related to market research. 
Moreover, due to the datasets that the LLM’s were trained on, the LLM may perpetuate existing social biases that can 
lead to discriminatory outcomes. It is crucial not to treat the simulation as absolute representations of customer 
behavior. This simulation only serves as a co-pilot for entrepreneurs and managers to seek more cost-efficient and 
faster ways to comprehend consumer behavior and WTP.
6. Conclusion
Results show that the use of LLMs, specifically ChatGPT, can simulate customer WTP to certain price ranges. This 
refers to the use of prompt engineering and CoT on GPT-3.5’s homo silicus characteristic, allowing it to replicate 
demand on the generated subjects. The results suggest that further iterations and studies must be performed to develop 
new ideas on the LLM’s capability of understanding complex human behavior. Without the need to train the model, 
GPT-3.5 can serve as a viable co-pilot to customer discovery for future business entrepreneurs, but more experiments 
must be performed to improve on its accuracy, leading to lower costs.
No customized pre-training or fine-tuning was conducted as the LLM relied on its stock knowledge to generate 
customer discovery simulations. Moreover, the LLM was tested only with the use of zero-shot and CoT prompting, 
as the simulation creators focused on ChatGPT’s inherent capability to detect and apply the theory of Veblen effects 
onto the business. Due to the nature of prompting, the lack of exposure to task-specific data may cause possible 
inaccuracies. Future work may include few-shot learning or fine-tuning in order for the LLM to generate more accurate 
and context-based customer discovery simulations. The researchers refrained from any explicit references to “Veblen 
goods” and the concept of non-intuitive demand curves in order to avoid helping the LLM, as the goal was to analyze 
its inherent knowledge on producing Veblen demand and WTP. Economic theory and “law of demand,” or any 
reference thereof, were omitted from the prompts to avoid further implications. However, by omitting these references, 
there is a risk of inadequately assessing the LLM’s capability to respond to the unique characteristics of Veblen goods.
Before applying prompt engineering in real-world settings, more research must be conducted on its ability to 
determine price point rationality, such as whether a product is deemed too expensive or cheap. Psychological human 
behavior can also be studied in the context of agentic and customer discovery simulation using ChatGPT as it can 
exhibit the usefulness in understanding more complex consumer behavior. This can be tested on qualitative feedback 
as it uses the LLM’s natural language processing (NLP) abilities. Future simulations can also be done using GPT-4, 
which allows for real-time web search, which may aid in providing a better context for the given business. The idea 
of Veblen can be tested on other industries as well. This could create a more refined insight on non-intuitive demand 
curves, which can be useful for economic discussions.
