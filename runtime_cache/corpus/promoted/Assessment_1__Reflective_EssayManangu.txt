Assessment 1: Reflective Essay 
 
For me, José Rizal’s journey to becoming a hero was not a mere coincidence. His intentional 
transformation, shaped by various factors such as family, social injustices, educational 
experiences, and strong moral values, transformed a boy from Calamba into the intellectual 
heart of a nation. Understanding what led to José Rizal’s transformation would serve as useful 
insight for personal development as well as education that produces socially responsible 
professionals. Through Rizal’s early life in Calamba, he has been brought up by family devotion, 
education, and cultural and environmental exposure to become an open-minded, disciplined, 
and compassionate individual. The qualities became the basis for Rizal’s future achievements 
as a writer, reformist, and national hero. Teodora Alonso, Rizal’s mother and his first teacher, is 
the core of these qualities. She taught Rizal to read, appreciate stories, and love literature, 
which nurtured his natural curiosity and sense of justice (Dalisay, 2026). It is a good point to 
consider that mentoring begins in our houses and what we do in public places will have 
consequences in our lives. 
Formal education and political engagement played a significant role in shaping Rizal's character 
beyond his family background. The need for justice and changes motivated him because of the 
influence of interaction with his brother Paciano, the unjust sacrifice of the Gomburza priests, 
and Jesuit education, which was morally responsible and highly critical. His mother's injustice 
only made his character even more dedicated (Dalisay, 2026). The intellectual horizon of Rizal 
greatly broadened as he finally managed to study in Europe. Along with his contribution to La 
Solidaridad, he wrote two political novels, Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo, having a huge 
impact on the Philippine nation. Together with López Jaena and Marcelo del Pilar, he became 
one of the key leaders of the Propaganda Movement (Britannica Editors, 2020). Instead of 
resorting to violence to show his anger, Rizal decided to turn it into writing, making his pen the 
most powerful weapon. This choice exemplified his heritage. Rather than resorting to violence to 
express his love for the Philippines, he utilised his knowledge, skills, and abilities to enlighten 
his fellow countrymen through peaceful means, including novels, essays, and articles (Grin, 
McFly International, Christopher F. Bueno, 2012). 
Reflecting on Rizal's experiences brings to light a significant truth about my own personal 
growth. In this case, I have realised that moving away from self-centredness is of great 
importance in terms of real development. Just like Rizal, I have realised that there is a need for 
me to open myself up and be aware of the fact that the education and skills I possess do not 
belong only to me, but instead, they are also a responsibility I owe to the community I serve. 
The path taken by Rizal shows me that the consistency between professional and moral 
qualities should always be a conscious effort.
