1.​EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CONTEXT  
Community Profile: 
The target participants for this service-learning project are Grade 5 learners of Porac Elementary School, Poblacion, Porac, 
Pampanga, through a virtual learning session. Grade 5 pupils are at a developmental stage where they are beginning to 
understand social issues, appreciate diverse perspectives, and develop stronger moral reasoning. This makes them an 
ideal audience for introducing Peace Education concepts such as empathy, respect, cooperation, and peaceful conflict 
resolution. The virtual format allows us to deliver meaningful and interactive learning experiences despite logistical 
constraints while ensuring accessibility for the learners. By engaging these students at an early age, nurturing their values 
and behaviors can contribute to a culture of peace within their school, families, and communities. 
Statement of Need:  
The findings of the case analysis on the 2013 Zamboanga City Crisis highlighted how conflict deeply affects communities, 
especially children who experience fear, trauma, and disrupted education. Although the crisis occurred in a different region, 
its lessons remain relevant because misunderstandings, prejudice, and conflicts can also arise in everyday school and 
community settings. As future educators, introducing peace education and character building at an early age helps learners 
develop empathy, respect for diversity, and peaceful conflict resolution skills. Grade 5 learners need meaningful 
opportunities to strengthen their literacy while learning to communicate respectfully, think critically, and resolve 
disagreements through dialogue rather than violence. Through storytelling, collaborative activities, and character-building 
experiences, learners can better appreciate unity, human rights, and responsible citizenship. Ultimately, this advocacy aims 
to empower learners to become compassionate peacemakers who help build more inclusive, respectful, and peaceful 
communities. 
 
 

Project Overview: 
The proposed service-learning intervention is a virtual Peace Education session for Grade 5 learners that integrates 
literacy activities with character-building and peace education. Through storytelling, guided discussions, and interactive 
collaborative activities, learners will develop empathy, respect for diversity, and peaceful conflict resolution skills while 
reflecting on how they can become peacemakers in their own school and community. 
 
2.​UN SDGS & PEACE EDUCATION INTEGRATION 
Primary SDG: SDG 4 – Quality Education 
The project supports SDG 4 by using storytelling, reading activities, literacy games, guided discussions, and learner 
activity sheets to strengthen the participants’ functional literacy and foundational learning skills. These activities will help 
improve their reading comprehension, vocabulary, communication, and ability to express their ideas. The donation of 
children’s books and school supplies will also provide additional learning resources that may support their educational 
needs. Through interactive and age-appropriate activities, the participants will be encouraged to listen, read, speak, and 
participate with confidence. In this way, the project promotes inclusive and engaging learning while supporting the 
development of essential literacy skills.  
Secondary SDG: SDG 16 – Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions 
The project supports SDG 16 by teaching participants how to communicate respectfully, listen to others, and resolve 
disagreements without violence. The stories, guided discussions, collaborative games, and digital advocacy materials 
will present situations that encourage fairness, cooperation, empathy, and peaceful decision-making. Participants will 
also be given opportunities to express their thoughts while respecting different opinions and experiences. Through these 
activities, they can learn that peace begins with simple actions such as listening, understanding, showing kindness, and 
treating others fairly. The project therefore helps promote peaceful dialogue, mutual respect, and harmony within the 
school and community.   
Core Values & Concepts:   
●​
Active listening – Participants will be taught to listen carefully without interrupting and to understand the message 
and feelings of others.  
●​
Empathy – The activities will help them recognize the experiences and emotions of other people and respond with 
care and understanding. 
●​
Respect for diversity – Participants will learn to value differences in culture, background, beliefs, abilities, and 
perspectives. 
●​
Nonviolent communication – They will practice expressing their feelings and concerns calmly, respectfully, and 
without using hurtful words or actions. 
 

●​
Cooperation and responsibility – Group activities will encourage participants to work together, share ideas, follow 
agreed rules, and take responsibility for their actions.

Delivery Modes:  
The team will first organize regular meetings to discuss the project plan, collect teaching materials, organize activity 
resources, and assign tasks. These face-to-face activities encourage participants to practice reading and writing skills while 
learning peaceful communication with one another.  
1.​ Donation Drive 
●​
A community-based donation drive encouraging the collection of pre-loved clothes, children's storybooks, 
school supplies, and hygiene kits to support selected learners while promoting compassion, generosity, and 
community solidarity.  
2.​ Multimedia Peace Advocacy Campaign 
●​
A digital advocacy campaign using social media platforms, infographics, short reels, and educational 
materials that promote peace education, empathy, kindness, respect, and dialogue. 
3.​ Virtual Literacy and Character-Building Workshop 
●​
An estimated two-hour virtual session featuring: interactive storytelling, peace discussions, literacy games, 
and reflection writing.

Ethical Protocol:  
The Risk Management and Ethical Considerations section outlines proactive strategies to address operational challenges 
and ensure the safety of participants during peace education activities. Operational risks such as technical failures, 
schedule delays, and emotional distress are managed using pre-downloaded media, audio, and strict timekeeping. Ethical 
protocols strictly enforce cultural sensitivity, age-appropriate content, and informed parental consent to protect student 
dignity and privacy. Through these combined efforts, the initiative lives up to the 'do no harm' principle, which guarantees a 
compassionate environment where participants can engage with complete peace of mind. 
 
6.​EVALUATION & REFLECTIVE WRITING FRAMEWORK 
 
●​
Evaluation Mode: After the implementation of the advocacy project, participating Grade 5 learners will complete 
a 5-item feedback survey to evaluate the effectiveness of the multimedia advocacy campaign, literacy and 
character-building workshop, and donation drive. The survey will assess learners' understanding of peace 
education concepts, engagement in the activities, and willingness to practice peaceful behaviors in their daily 
lives. The survey statements will have choices that indicate their response level from Disagree to Strongly Agree. 
Sample Survey Questions 
1.​ The stories and activities helped me understand the importance of peace, kindness, and respect. 
2.​ I learned that conflicts can be resolved through dialogue instead of violence. 
3.​ The activities encouraged me to respect and appreciate people who are different from me. 
4.​ I feel more confident resolving disagreements peacefully after participating in today's activities. 
5.​ I enjoyed participating in today's peace education activities and would like to join similar activities again. 
●​
Individual Reflective Writing: After the schedule of the action plan has been approved, every member will 
 

write a 150-word reflection in a Royal Blue Cattleya filler notebook describing their experiences, contributions, 
lessons learned, and growth as peacebuilders and future educators. 
●​
Group Debrief: The group debrief is to be accomplished after each meeting for 20 minutes to discuss their 
reflections, accomplishments, challenges encountered, and as such.
