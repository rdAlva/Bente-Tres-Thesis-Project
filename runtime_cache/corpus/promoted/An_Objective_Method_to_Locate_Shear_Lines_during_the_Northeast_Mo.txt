Abstract

24 
The shear line is a narrow zone of maximum horizontal wind shear, typically identified as a 
25 
confluence zone of low-level wind streams. Previous studies showed that this system can trigger 
26 
heavy to extreme rainfall events in the Philippines during the northeast monsoon season. However, 
27 
a research gap remains in objectively identifying and locating this system. Thus, this study 
28 
develops a detection method that may be used for monitoring and forecasting the location of shear 
29 
lines. Results show that the gradient of meridional winds in the y-direction (
𝜕𝜕𝑉𝑉925ℎ𝑃𝑃𝑃𝑃
𝜕𝜕𝜕𝜕
 ), the boundary 
30 
layer moisture flux convergence, and the 925 hPa relative vorticity may be used to estimate the 
31 
location of the shear line. These parameters are then utilized in the detection algorithm. Applying 
32 
the detection algorithm to reanalysis data from 1979 to 2022 for the northeast monsoon season 
33 
revealed that shear lines contribute up to 20% of the total extreme rainfall days, with the greatest 
34 
influence observed in November and December. This system is also best depicted in the 925 hPa 
35 
and 850 hPa levels. The detection method developed in this study may be useful for improving the 
36 
forecast of heavy to extreme rainfall events in the Philippines, especially during this season.

1. Introduction 
41 
During the southwest monsoon season in the Philippines (May to September), tropical cyclones 
42 
(TCs; Cinco et al. 2016; Bagtasa 2017; Basconcillo and Moon 2020; Petilla et al. 2025), low-
43 
pressure systems (LPS) that are weaker than TCs (Olaguera et al. 2023), the Boreal Summer Intra-
44 

Olaguera et al., Objective Identification of Shear Lines in the Philippines 
 
3 
Seasonal Oscillation (BSISO; Olaguera et al. 2022), and enhanced southwest monsoon by TCs 
45 
(Cayanan et al. 2011; Bathan et al. 2025) are reported to contribute to rainfall extremes in this 
46 
season. For example, during the active and strong phases of the BSISO, the probability of extreme 
47 
rainfall occurrence in the Philippines can increase to as much as 80% in some regions. The active 
48 
phases of the BSISO can also facilitate the development of more TCs over the western North 
49 
Pacific that will eventually affect the Philippines. On the other hand, systems such as LPS 
50 
(Olaguera et al. 2023), the Madden Julian Oscillation (MJO; Pullen et al. 2015; Bagtasa et al. 2020; 
51 
Olaguera et al. 2024a), cold surges (Lim et al. 2017; Abdillah et al. 2022), and shear lines 
52 
(Olaguera and Matsumoto 2020; Olaguera et al. 2021a, b; Reyes et al. 2024) contribute to the 
53 
rainfall extremes during the northeast monsoon season (November to March), particularly along 
54 
the eastern coast of the Philippines. Olaguera et al. (2024) quantified the contribution of MJO to 
55 
be as much as 16% of the rainfall extremes in some stations along the eastern coast of the country 
56 
in this season. TCs also contribute to the extremes during the northeast monsoon season (e.g., 
57 
Espinueva et al. 2012; Petilla et al. 2025), although they are less frequent compared to those during 
58 
the southwest monsoon season (e.g., Cinco et al. 2016; Bagtasa 2017). 
59 
A shear line is a narrow zone of maximum horizontal wind shear, typically identified by a 
60 
confluence of air streams on the equatorward side of a col (Ramage 1995; Pielke 2013). This zone 
61 
is termed as a shear line because the winds across it may blow in opposite directions or display 
62 
sharp differences in horizontal wind speed (Pielke 2013). A col represents a saddle-like pressure 
63 
pattern, located between two low-pressure systems and two high-pressure systems 
64 
(https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/Col). Shear lines commonly form when mid-latitude cold fronts 
65 
intrude into the tropics, where their thickness gradients weaken upon encountering the warm 
66 
tropical oceans. Low-level convergence, aided by convective activity, may persist during the 
67 

Olaguera et al., Objective Identification of Shear Lines in the Philippines 
 
4 
conversion process of the cold front to a shear line (Yokoi and Matsumoto 2008; Pielke 2013). It 
68 
can also interact with a low-level cyclonic vortex in the central and southern Philippines, which 
69 
hinders its southward propagation (Olaguera et al. 2021; Reyes et al. 2024). Therefore, determining 
70 
the location of the shear line is crucial for identifying areas that are at risk of heavy and extreme 
71 
rainfall.  
72 
However, the impact and mechanism of this system have only been recently examined by a few 
73 
studies (Faustino-Eslava et al. 2011; Olaguera and Matsumoto 2020; Olaguera et al. 2021a,b; 
74 
Reyes et al. 2024) despite the catastrophic impact that they may bring. Reyes et al. (2024) recently 
75 
examined the heavy rainfall events (HREs) during the northeast monsoon season in the Philippines. 
76 
They identified three patterns of HREs associated with a strong East Asian Winter Monsoon and 
77 
the interaction of a shear line with a low-level cyclonic vortex in the vicinity of the Philippines. 
78 
However, there is no available index or method that can be used to locate the shear line objectively. 
79 
Therefore, the purpose of this study is to develop a detection algorithm that may be used for 
80 
monitoring the shear line. This detection method will also be useful for quantifying the 
81 
contribution of shear lines to the rainfall extremes during the northeast monsoon season in the 
82 
Philippines, for identifying areas that are more likely to experience heavy to extreme rainfall events, 
83 
and for improving our understanding of how this system evolves. The rest of the paper is organized 
84 
as follows. The data used in this study are briefly presented in Section 2. The development of the 
85 
shear line detection algorithm is then discussed in Section 3. The climatological features of shear 
86 
lines and their contribution to rainfall extremes in the Philippines are described in Section 4. 
87 
Finally, summary and concluding remarks are presented in Section 5.

3.2. Detecting the shear line 
157 
The shear line is detected through the following steps, which is also shown in Figure S6. For 
158 
illustrative purposes, we demonstrate the detection of the shear line at 06 UTC on January 16, 
159 
2017.  
160 
(a) First, the candidate points per longitude are identified within 115–135°E, 5–22°N. This 
161 
domain is similar to the Philippine Area of Responsibility but with a small latitudinal extent 
162 
(i.e., 22°N instead of 25°N). This domain was used to filter out the shear lines that are not 
163 
directly influencing the Philippines, such as those near Taiwan between 22–25°N. The 
164 
candidate points are the latitude coordinates with at least   
𝜕𝜕𝑉𝑉925ℎ𝑃𝑃𝑃𝑃
𝜕𝜕𝜕𝜕
 ≤ –1 × 10–5 s–1, 
165 
VIMFC > 0, and RV925hPa > 0. The VIMFC condition was added since we are only interested 
166 
in rainfall inducing shear lines. The 
𝜕𝜕𝑉𝑉925ℎ𝑃𝑃𝑃𝑃
𝜕𝜕𝜕𝜕
 threshold value was determined by trial and 
167 
error. A sensitivity test of the results to this parameter is discussed in Supplement 2.  
168 
(b) Second, the candidate grid points were grouped using a simple clustering by proximity (i.e., 
169 
Single-Linkage clustering; Martinez and Martinez, 2005). A justification for using this 
170 
clustering methodology is discussed in Supplement 2. Each coordinate is assigned to an 
171 
initial empty cluster. Then, their distances from one another are computed using the 
172 
Haversine formula. When the distance of one coordinate from another is less than 2.5° (2 
173 

Olaguera et al., Objective Identification of Shear Lines in the Philippines 
 
9 
grid points or ~277.5 km for the JRA55 data set), they are assigned to the same cluster. 
174 
Otherwise, a new cluster is created, and the distances are recomputed. This procedure is 
175 
looped through all the candidate coordinates. Finally, the clusters with at least 10 degrees 
176 
longitude in extent are obtained. This will filter out the short shear lines. 
177 
(c) Third, the clusters in (b) that are at least 555 km (5°) from the zero contour line of the 925 
178 
hPa meridional wind are extracted. This was added to the condition since the shear line is 
179 
close to this zero contour line, as shown in Figure 2a. 
180 
(d) A 1-dimensional Gaussian filter is then applied to both filtered latitude and longitude points 
181 
in (c). We used a value of 3 for the standard deviation of the Gaussian function, which 
182 
controls the smoothing of the shear line.

4. Climatology of detected shear lines and their contribution to extreme rainfall 
196 
Figure 5 shows the density map of the detected shear lines from 1979 to 2022. A total of 2,432 
197 
days with shear lines (i.e., shear line days) were detected, which is about 36.5% of the total days 
198 
(6,504 days). About 51%, 46%, 38%, 30%, and 22% of the total days in November, December, 
199 
January, February, and March, have shear lines that are within the domain in Figure 5, 
200 
respectively. Spatially, shear lines appear more frequently to the north of 10°N, particularly in 
201 
November and December, compared to its southern region. We also counted the “shear line 
202 
events,” where shear lines were tracked across space and time, with a unique ID assigned to 
203 
consecutive occurrence in time at least 555 km (5°) apart (i.e., this identifies the same shear line that is 
204 
propagating in space in time and assigned with only one unique ID). This yielded 17, 14, 13, 11, and 11 
205 
shear line events per month for November, December, January, February, and March, respectively. 
206 
The contribution of shear lines to extreme rainfall along the eastern coast of the Philippines was 
207 
examined. A shear line is considered to have contributed to the extreme rainfall days in a station 
208 
if it is located within a 150 km radius centered at the station location. The percentage contribution 
209 
is the ratio of the extreme rainfall days associated with the shear lines to the total number of 
210 
extreme rainfall days, expressed as a percentage in each station (Table 1). Overall, shear lines 
211 
account for up to 20% of the total number of extreme rainfall days. Specifically, stations in Luzon 
212 
(Casiguran, Infanta, Daet, Alabat, Tayabas, Virac, and Legazpi City) experience higher extreme 
213 
rainfall days triggered by the shear line during November and December, while those in the 
214 

Olaguera et al., Objective Identification of Shear Lines in the Philippines 
 
11 
Visayas (Catarman and Guiuan) and Mindanao (Surigao City) experience more extreme rainfall 
215 
days from November to January. A higher percentage of extreme days associated with shear lines 
216 
occurs in January at Hinatuan station in Mindanao, which is the southernmost station along the 
217 
eastern coast and the wettest location in the Philippines (Olaguera and Manalo, 2024a).

Mindanao). Furthermore, shear lines account for no more than 20% of the total incidence of 
239 
extreme rainfall days observed at eastern coastal stations in the Philippines. To test the 
240 
effectiveness of the detection algorithm, we demonstrate that it can be applied to a forecast of a 
241 
heavy rainfall event in the Philippines, as explained in Supplement 3. The algorithm was able to 
242 
depict the forecast position of the shear line that induced heavy rainfall along the eastern coast of 
243 
the Philippines on January 9, 2025.  
244 
There are still some areas to explore in future work. As mentioned in the discussion, only 72% of 
245 
the shear line days analyzed by Reyes et al. (2024) were detected by the developed detection 
246 
algorithm. Perhaps additional criteria such as the gradient of the divergence field (e.g., Berry and 
247 
Reeder, 2014) may be added to improve the detection of shear lines. The performance of the 
248 
detection algorithm on high-resolution datasets also needs to be tested further in future works. As 
249 
demonstrated in Supplement 3, the smoothing function was adjusted since the wind fields are 
250 
noisier in the high-resolution data. The precise evolution of the shear line and its interaction with 
251 
other systems were not examined in detail in this study. Furthermore, not all shear lines may cause 
252 
heavy to extreme rainfall in the Philippines. The conditions responsible for shear line-induced 
253 
heavy to extreme rainfall warrant further investigation.

SUPPLEMENT 2

Additional information on the detection algorithm 
46 
In this section, we discuss the methodology and the sensitivity tests that we conducted in 
47 
developing the detection algorithm. Determining the appropriate radius for estimating the extreme 
48 
rainfall contribution of shear lines is also discussed. 
49 
S2.1. The flowchart of the shear line detection algorithm 
50 
 
51 
Figure S6. The methodology for estimating the location of the shear line. 
52 
As outlined in Section 3 in the main text, the shear line was detected through the following steps, 
53 
as shown in Figure S6. The 06 UTC on January 16, 2017 was used as a test case.  
54 

Olaguera et al., Objective Identification of Shear Lines in the Philippines 
 
7 
Step 1: First, the candidate points per longitude are identified within 115–135°E, 5–22°N. 
55 
These candidate points are the latitude coordinates with at least   
𝜕𝜕𝑉𝑉925ℎ𝑃𝑃𝑃𝑃
𝜕𝜕𝜕𝜕
 ≤ –1 × 10–5 s–
56 
1, VIMFC > 0, and RV925hPa > 0. These candidate grid points were then grouped using a 
57 
simple clustering by proximity (i.e., Single-Linkage clustering). Then, their distances from 
58 
one another are computed using the Haversine formula. When the distance of one 
59 
coordinate from another is less than 2.5° (2 grid points or ~277.5 km), they are assigned to 
60 
the same cluster. Otherwise, a new cluster is created, and the distances are recomputed. 
61 
This procedure is looped through all the candidate coordinates. Finally, the shear lines with 
62 
at least 10 degrees longitude are obtained. 
63 
Step 2: The clusters in (b) that are close to the zero contour line of the 925 hPa meridional 
64 
winds are extracted.  
65 
Step 3: A 1-dimensional Gaussian filter was then applied to filtered latitude and longitude 
66 
points obtained in Step 2.  
67 
S2.2. The choice of the clustering technique  
68 
We tested another clustering method, the Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with 
69 
Noise (DBSCAN; Ester et al., 1996; Schubert et al., 2017), in addition to the Single-Linkage 
70 
Clustering (SLC). This method is used for spatial clustering and can cluster points with different 
71 
shapes and densities. However, two key input parameters require tuning when using this method: 
72 
the radius of the neighborhood (eps parameter) and the minimum number of points required to 
73 
form a dense region (minpts). For the January 16, 2017, case, we found that eps = 2 and minpts = 
74 
2 are enough to detect the shear line on this date. These values may vary per year and need to be 
75 
estimated when applied to climatological data. This will not be practical for forecasting 
76 

Olaguera et al., Objective Identification of Shear Lines in the Philippines 
 
8 
applications. The k-means clustering, on the other hand, which is another popular clustering 
77 
technique, assumes that the shape of the clusters is spherical, which is not appropriate for detecting 
78 
shear lines. The SLC used in this study does not require the eps and minpts conditions, unlike 
79 
DBSCAN. The SLC method may also be used for clustering elongated or chain-like clusters, 
80 
whose density may not be enough to be recognized by DBSCAN.  
81

85

86 
study on January 16, 2017, the region with  
𝜕𝜕𝑉𝑉925ℎ𝑃𝑃𝑃𝑃
𝜕𝜕𝜕𝜕
 ≤ –1 × 10–5 s–1 coincides with the region 
87 
with VIMFC > 0. The VIMFC condition is important for filtering the rainfall-inducing shearlines.

S2.4. Inclusion of 925 hPa Relative Vorticity (RV925hPa) in the criteria
