Is Face-to-Face better than Online Classes?
Our planet, as we all know, is in trouble. We live in a time where
normality appears to be non-existent. Stores are closed, people are sick,
and education will be delivered entirely online for the remainder of the
semester. My heart sank when I heard that students should not return to
campus, instead, do their work online. MDSF has became my home, and I
wanted nothing more than to finish my high school life the same way I had
begun it in a classroom with all of my friends and that one teacher we all
adored. Having said that, many students are not as connected online as
they are in person. I agree that face-to-face learning provides students with
immediate pleasure in terms of answers and assistance. This is when the
teacher and the student meet in a specific location and time for one-on-one
learning or, more often, group class sessions similar to what happens in
school. Face-to-face learning is a very successful method of acquiring
knowledge and skills since it frequently incorporates many modes of
learning such as writing, reading, conversation, presentations, projects,
group work, video clips, demonstrations, and practice.
When a student is in class, they might ask questions or seek
assistance with a specific difficulty. If the student was at home, they would
have to wait for the teacher to respond through email. Face-to-face
learning, as opposed to computer-based education, provides a personal
touch to a session. Other advantages of face-to-face learning are that you'll
be able to concentrate harder on your learning because there’ll be fewer
distractions than if you were at home, and if you are online, there are so
many distractions. The typical downsides of online learning revolve around
technical issues. Nothing ruins an online class more than problems with
audio, video, or connectivity. Excessive screen time can cause a variety of
physical problems, such as bad posture and headaches. The internet is
designed to divert students' attention away from studying content by
providing social media and entertainment just a click away. The best online
learning platforms and software available include a variety of tools and
features to assist students in being attentive and interested. Let us be

honest, none of us can sit through an hour of class without becoming
distracted or exhausted. Sometimes you need to close your eyes, yawn, or
gaze out the window. What is the point of sitting in front of a computer for
an extended amount of time, with just brief intervals, for half of the day?
The majority of students will remain silent for the majority of the lesson and
will still have to go over all of the content on their own.
Yes, it is true that the world is evolving where we must update our
learning techniques amidst pandemic and embrace fresh approaches and
be open to novel ideas but I do not think this is for everyone. This approach
only favors the privileged people who are capable of keeping themselves
focused on every subject because they have nothing else to do except
study. I want to point out that while both forms of education have their own
advantages and disadvantages, face-to-face education is more trustworthy
and superior. Learning in groups and face-to-face helps students to
broaden their horizons. It allows them to build a rapport with the instructor
and boost their confidence. Looking into the camera for hours without our
minds being focused on because of the different distractions at home
results in practically nothing and is not a viable approach.
