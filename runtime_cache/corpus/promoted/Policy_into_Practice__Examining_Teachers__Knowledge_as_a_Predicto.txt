Policy into Practice: Examining Teachers’ Knowledge as a Predictor of Teachers’ 
Attitude Towards Child Protection Classroom Integration 
 
 
 
Floro T. del Pilar III 
floro.delpilar@deped.gov.ph 
Guinayang National High School 
Ateneo de Manila University 
 
 
The Department of Education issued DepEd Order no. 40 s.2012 or Child Protection 
Policy (CPP) to protect children and provide a safe learning environment (Casipe & Bete, 
2023). Research indicates that teachers' awareness and positive attitudes towards CPP are 
important for effective implementation in schools (Bayucca, 2020; dela Fuente, 2021). 
Previous studies have examined the level of knowledge of teachers about CPP and the 
teachers’ attitude towards CPP separately (Asio et al., 2020; Bayucca, 2020; Mag-atas & 
Carmona, 2023). However, there is a dearth of studies examining whether the teachers’ 
knowledge of child protection policy predicts their attitude towards integrating child 
protection policy into their classroom practices. 
 
This study aimed to examine the teachers’ knowledge of child protection policy as a 
predictor of their attitude towards integrating the policy into their classroom practices. The 
study hypothesized that the higher the level of knowledge the teachers have about child 
protection policy, the better their attitude is towards integrating CPP into their classroom 
practices. 

 
Method 
Participants 
This study has 159 teacher participants randomly selected through stratified sampling 
from the 3 chosen junior high schools of the District of San Mateo, Division of Rizal, Region 
4A for the school year 2023-24. The schools that participated in the study are the largest in 
the district according to the number of teachers, with each having more than 70 teachers. The 
sample consists of 33.33% from School A, 32.08% from School B, and 34.59% from school 
C. The Cochran finite population correction formula was used to determine the appropriate 
sample size population.  
Measures 
Teachers’ Knowledge of Child Protection Policy (CPP). To measure the teachers’ 
knowledge of CPP into their classroom practices, participants were asked their level of 
knowledge on (a) the purpose of DepEd Order No. 40 s. 2012, (b) the different forms of child 
abuse and neglect according to the CPP, (c) the different signs of child abuse and neglect 
according to the CPP, (d) the reporting procedures for suspected cases of child abuse or 
neglect, (e) the roles and responsibilities of a teacher in CPP, (f) the prevention strategies for 
child abuse or neglect, (g) provide CPP-sensitive examples for the students, (h) the 
intervention strategies for child abuse or neglect, (i) the different ways to integrate CPP into 
my teaching principles, and (j) the provisions for ensuring the confidentiality and privacy of 
information related to child abuse cases. Items were rated on a 5-point scale, with 1 as not 
knowledgeable, 2 as limited knowledge, 3 as moderate knowledge, 4 as knowledgeable, and 5 
as highly knowledgeable. 

 
Teachers’ Attitude Towards Integrating Child Protection Policy (CPP) into their 
Classroom Practices. To measure the teachers’ attitude towards integrating of CPP into their 
classroom practices, participants were asked if (a) integrating CPP in their classroom is 
important, (b) they have confidence that integrating CPP into their lessons will contribute to 
its successful implementation, (c) the time and effort they use to integrate CPP into their 
lessons make them a better teacher, (d) integrating CPP into their classrooms will benefit 
their students, (e) the CPP is relevant to the subject/s that they teach, (f) the CPP inclusion 
improves the value of their lesson, (g) integrating CPP makes their lessons more relevant and 
engaging for their students, (h) integrating CPP into their lessons will create a safe and 
supportive learning environment, (i) integrating CPP in their lessons will develop necessary 
life skills in their students, and (j) integrating CPP in their lessons aligns with their values as 
a teacher. Items were rated on a 5-point scale, with 1 as strongly disagree, 2 as disagree, 3 as 
neutral, 4 as agree, and 5 as strongly agree. 
Data Analysis 
 
To test the hypothesis, the simple linear regression was used predicting the teachers’ 
attitude towards integrating CPP into their classroom practices with their level of knowledge 
of CPP. 
Results 
 
A linear regression analysis was conducted to examine the relationship between 
teachers’ knowledge of CPP and their attitude towards integrating CPP into their classroom 
practices. The Shapiro-Wilk test indicated that the data deviates from normality (W= <.001). 
However, given the large sample size (n=159), the central limit theorem applies. The q-plot 
showed approximate normality although some deviations are found in the tails. The 

 
assumptions of normality using the linearity, homoscedasticity and Cook’s distances were 
satisfied. 
 
The overall model was significant, F(1, 157) = 90.2, p <.001, R2 = 0.365. Knowledge 
of CPP explained 36.5% of the variance in the attitude towards integrating CPP into 
classroom practices. The unstandardized regression coefficient for knowledge was B = 0.576, 
SE = 0.0606, t = 9.50, p < .001, indicating that for every unit increase in knowledge, the 
attitude increases by 0.576 points. The standardized regression coefficient was β = 0.604, 
suggesting a positive and strong effect size. These findings suggest that higher level of 
knowledge about CPP is associated with better attitude towards integrating CPP into 
classroom practices. 
Discussion 
 
This work highlights that the teachers’ knowledge of CPP is a significant predictor of 
attitude towards integrating CPP into their classroom practices. As a predictor, the study 
showed that knowledge of CPP explains a substantial portion of the variance of teachers’ 
attitudes towards its integration into their classrooms. This aligns with Asio et al. (2020) 
which found that teachers’ knowledge of CPP significantly improved their attitudes toward 
reporting suspected child abuse. 
 
School policy makers must draft seminar workshops that emphasize CPP knowledge 
and its applications to encourage practical integrations in classroom practices. Strengthened 
knowledge-based interventions leads to better policy adherence which ultimately leads to 
safe learning environments for students. Other factors that influence teachers’ attitudes 

 
toward CPP integration must be studied to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the 
factors that builds teachers’ attitudes toward CPP integration in classrooms.
