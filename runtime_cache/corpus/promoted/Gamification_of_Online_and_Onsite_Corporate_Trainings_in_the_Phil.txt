Abstract. This study examined Filipino corporate trainers’ views on the effec-
tiveness of gamiﬁcation in onsite and online training. Using Clark and Braun’s 
thematic analysis, eight trainers shared their insights into its impact on engage-
ment, motivation, and reinforcement. Results showed gamiﬁcation can boost par-
ticipation and structure learning, but its success depends on deliberate integration 
of game elements and digital tools. This varied slightly between modalities, espe-
cially when involving inductive and deductive assessments. Trainers emphasized 
the importance of incorporating interactive activities, providing feedback, enhanc-
ing competition, and offering rewards to strengthen engagement. The study also 
identiﬁed the complementary beneﬁts of online and onsite training when combined 
with gamiﬁcation techniques in a human-computer interaction setup. However, a 
strategic approach is crucial when aligning gamiﬁcation with learning objectives.

Background of the Study 
Organizations are continually exploring various online and onsite training strategies 
to enhance workforce skills and performance [9]. One approach is through gamiﬁca-
tion, which involves incorporating game design elements such as rewards, rankings, 
and achievements into training programs [10]. It has become increasingly prevalent in 
contributing to employee engagement and knowledge retention [22]. However, while 
widely adopted, gamiﬁcation remains a strategy that has yet to be fully explored and 
developed. 
Corporate training in the Philippines has continued to utilize both on-site and online 
training methods, even after the COVID-19 pandemic. This study examined how Filipino 
corporate trainers perceive the effectiveness of gamiﬁcation in training programs. It also 
explored the challenges and opportunities that corporate trainers face when integrating 
and scaling gamiﬁcation strategies, as well as how emerging technologies, particularly 
artiﬁcial intelligence, can contribute to these strategies and assess their adaptability 
within a corporate training landscape.

Review of Related Literature 
2.1 
Corporate Training and Gamiﬁcation 
Digital change presents both opportunities and challenges for corporate trainers, necessi-
tating enhanced communication, reﬁned instructional design methodologies, and align-
ment with employees’ preferred learning styles [25]. The trend toward corporate e-
learning is emphasized as a means of increasing worker productivity and ﬂexibility, 
supporting a well-rounded plan that combines online and traditional training method-
ologies [9]. A crucial area is virtual onboarding, where ensuring a smooth transition into 
corporate culture depends on effective communication, the formation of digital work 
connections, and the availability of appropriate tools [26]. 
Artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) is particularly revolutionizing corporate digital transfor-
mation by enhancing learning and development through personalized training, AI-driven 
coaching, and immersive technologies, while also optimizing human resource functions 
[16]. AI-powered corporate training aligns with Industry 4.0 standards by incorporat-
ing ethical principles such as accountability, privacy, and equity while leveraging sys-
tems theory, psychology, and economics to enhance training effectiveness and human-
centered learning [3, 7]. Despite these training improvements, virtual learning still faces 
challenges with participation and teamwork, which can be mitigated by incorporating 
structured interactions and emotional intelligence components [24]. The application of 
virtual training enhances performance and creativity among employees [1]. 
Gamiﬁcation incorporates game elements into corporate training programs and other 
learning and development interventions, boosting engagement that further strengthens 
learner motivation by providing feedback on small achievements, which helps keep 
professionals focused on their training goals [18]. It enhances training effectiveness 
by leveraging technologies such as AI, which can personalize learning, create adaptive 
environments, and improve employee performance [10]. Despite these outcomes, gaps 
remain in exploring the long-term effectiveness of virtual training, as many evaluations 
focus on immediate learning gains rather than overall behavioral changes and business 
improvements [19]. 
The integration of game-like elements engages employees, leading to improved 
learning outcomes through emerging methods that incorporate gamiﬁcation into train-
ing programs [20, 22]. Common elements used in this design process include leader-
boards, rewards, narratives, and personalization, which have become increasingly preva-
lent in redesigning educational and corporate onsite training to increase participation 
[6, 22]. This method has gained traction with advancements in technology, creating 
an immersive learning environment in a dynamic business setting [10]. Rapid digital-
ization requires trainers to adopt new approaches, particularly through visualization 
techniques, to enhance efﬁciency in skills training, as the current technology-driven 
workplace demands [23, 26]. 
2.2 
Training Impact and Employee Development 
Effective corporate design integrates various methodologies and perspectives from train-
ers to enhance employee development and performance [4]. Implementing pedagogi-
cal interventions effectively enhances employees’ emotional intelligence, highlighting

its crucial role in enabling better teamwork and adaptability [14]. Connecting diverse 
people across departments and communities involves cooperative learning [20]. There-
fore, trainers should stay updated as they facilitate cross-departmental collaboration and 
knowledge sharing [15, 17]. 
Given the direct impact of structured training programs on an organization’s progress, 
training initiatives help employees reﬁne their skills and behaviors, creating a more 
productive work environment [8, 12]. Training contributes to corporate entrepreneurship 
by enhancing employees’ strategic decision-making skills and promoting innovation in 
business models and processes, thereby enabling them to remain competitive in the 
market [11]. In this regard, on-the-job training is crucial for developing employees’ 
technical and managerial skills, allowing them to learn by performing key tasks, develop 
expertise under supervision, and acquire interpersonal and problem-solving skills for 
effective workplace performance [11, 13]. 
Meanwhile, advancements in digital tools and AI-powered systems further enhance 
the impact of training, as AI-generated negative performance feedback can positively 
affect employee motivation and improve performance more effectively than human-
delivered feedback [21]. Learning management systems can provide structured feedback 
that helps improve goal setting and learning outcomes in corporate training [8]. 
2.3 
Synthesis 
A knowledge gap in corporate digital training and gamiﬁcation stems from limited inter-
disciplinary integration, as current research lacks insights from the theoretical underpin-
nings of psychology, education, and ethics. While gamiﬁcation strategies are expected to 
enhance corporate training, ﬁndings highlight the short-term beneﬁts that limit the under-
standing of how these strategies inﬂuence employee engagement, motivation, and knowl-
edge retention [8]. Understanding how gamiﬁcation strategies can integrate into existing 
training systems may help maximize their potential and overcome resistance to change. 
Technological developments can serve as potential learning scaffolds for many organi-
zations. The effectiveness and adaptability of technology-integrated gamiﬁcation strate-
gies in training, alongside conventional gamiﬁcation methods, remain underexplored, 
particularly from the perspective of corporate trainers based in the Philippines.

3.1 
Propositions of the Study 
1. Integrating technology and gamiﬁcation enhances the adaptability of corporate train-
ing programs across both on-site and online modalities by creating more engaging 
and compelling learning experiences. 
2. Integrating game-like elements into corporate training programs can signiﬁcantly 
enhance individual employee engagement and motivation, especially when combined 
with different training methodologies. 
3. A well-rounded training plan that integrates technology-supplemented gamiﬁed 
activities fosters team engagement and social interaction in corporate learning 
environments. 
4 
Methodology 
The study was conducted with eight Filipino corporate trainers through recorded Zoom 
video calls. Each individual in-depth interview lasted no more than 45 min. The chosen 
respondents have utilized gamiﬁcation strategies in their programs and possess relevant 
experience in both onsite and online training modalities. 
This study employed a descriptive and exploratory approach. For the descriptive 
aspect, the study describes the experiences, perspectives, and insights of Filipino corpo-
rate trainers regarding the use of gamiﬁcation and technology in both onsite and online 
training modalities. It seeks to understand how they perceive the impact of these elements 
on various aspects of employee development. Furthermore, given the evolving nature 
of digital transformation in corporate learning and the growing focus on gamiﬁcation 
within the corporate training industry, this exploratory study examines the nuances and 
complexities of these phenomena from the perspectives of Filipino corporate trainers. It 
seeks to uncover patterns, preferences, challenges, and even functional practices in the 
Philippine setting. 
The research instrument used for this study is a structured interview consisting of four 
parts. Questions revolved around the participants’ experiences with onsite and online 
training programs, the role of technology and gamiﬁcation in corporate training, and their 
perceived impact on employee engagement, motivation, and learning reinforcement. 
Given this, the study uses thematic analysis patterned from Braun and Clarke (2022), 
which involves an organic and comprehensive process: ﬁrst familiarizing with the inter-
view data by highlighting relevant keywords and quotations, then identifying codes

while maintaining the depth of trainers’ perspectives. 
5 
Discussion of Results 
The study validated that gamiﬁcation, supported by digital tools, has created more engag-
ing and adaptable learning experiences. As supported by the corporate trainers, they 
observed higher participation when interactive elements were consistently integrated. 
Corporate Trainer 8 expressed, “When I am able to make the program interactive and 
enjoyable, I notice that there’s a higher level of participation and completion rate.” 
Such responses highlight how the use of technology in modern gamiﬁcation offers new 
heights of opportunities for gauging participation and performance. The same corpo-
rate trainer emphasized, “because of technology and digitalization, training progress 
can now be more easily monitored and personalized.” Corporate Trainer 2 speciﬁcally 
brought up the tools they often use like “Mentimeter, Kahoot, and Google Slides”  at  the  
same time emphasizing that, for them, “breakout rooms in Zoom are very critical”  for  
encouraging group collaboration via online engagement. This sentiment extends across 
modalities, providing the convenience of remote learning, with Corporate Trainer 8 not-
ing that “digitalization has had a great inﬂuence positively on how I design my training 
programs for on-site training.” This demonstrates that gamiﬁcation augmented by tech-
nology crosses the boundary between online and onsite learning to create more effective 
and productive learning environments in both contexts.
Beyond implementation considerations, the study identiﬁed advantages unique with 
what technology-integrated gamiﬁcation offers. Corporate Trainer 8 quotes that when 
“participants can receive instant feedback, it helps them assess their progress and adjust 
accordingly.” Rather than positioning online and onsite training as opposing approaches, 
determining which is better than the other, the ﬁndings suggest that they offer comple-
mentary beneﬁts that can be combined. Corporate Trainer 5 expressed that “there are 
pros and cons to both. And deciding on which mode to use depends on the client objective, 
as well as the learning objective.” They further noted, “nowadays, it seems there’s no 
limit to what you can do really in training, whether it’s on site or online, because of these 
technology advancements and digital integration.” Given that, for future developments, 
participants showed enthusiasm for emerging technologies, with Corporate Trainer 8 
predicting “a strong shift towards highly personalized learning…with AI adapting the 
content and the assessment just to ﬁt the individual needs.” They also look forward to 
the possibility of how “virtual (VR) and augmented reality (AR)” will “revolutionize 
experiential learning.” This encourages corporate trainers and organizations to contin-
ually evolve and invest in their training programs to achieve better corporate learning 
outcomes. Hence, with the growing importance of technology in corporate training, it 
not only shapes how programs are delivered but also inﬂuences the tools and strategies 
that trainers utilize.

Building on this, the study identiﬁed the speciﬁc game-like elements that can foster 
both engagement and motivation among employees. Indeed, the integration of competi-
tive, game-like features goes beyond entertainment purposes, as it can signiﬁcantly inﬂu-
ence and enhance the learning experience as well. One of the most common approaches 
of corporate trainers involves the use of point systems and prizes, whether in online or 
onsite training. Corporate Trainer 2 explained, “I usually use point systems [onsite]…for 
example there’s an activity and usually the organizations would have sponsorship on 
prizes. So deﬁnitely we need to craft points for those things so that at least they’re moti-
vated to enjoy the activity.” Similarly, Corporate Trainer 7 highlighted the importance 
of incentives, stating that “…the game-like elements that are most effective [online] 
would be incentives,” which can include virtual rewards. These ﬁndings reveal how such 
reward systems can sustain employee interest and participation throughout a training 
program. However, it is important to note that the impact of these strategies was found 
to be often limited to the period of the training itself, as Corporate Trainer 4 pointed out, 
“I’m not involved in the post-program already,” which brings about questions regard-
ing their long-term impact on employee behavior and development once the program 
is ﬁnished. In addition to points and prizes, elements such as leaderboards and compe-
tition were also noted for their role in driving employee motivation and performance. 
As Corporate Trainer 8 observed, “…this helps me reinforce positive behavior and it 
keeps the motivation levels high…it adds a competitive element, so it drives the par-
ticipants to perform better.” The effectiveness of these game-like elements, however, 
seemingly depends on how well and purposefully they are integrated into the overall 
training design—combined with other methodologies or strategies. Corporate Trainer 5 
speciﬁcally emphasized this point, stating that gamiﬁcation is “good to grab attention 
and to keep attention, but whether or not it can deepen the learning, I think it should be 
coupled with other strategies.” The same corporate trainer also expressed apprehension 
about “how gamiﬁcation should be used for more in-depth learning rather than surface 
attention-grabbing objectives.” Therefore, when designing effective training programs, 
it is essential for corporate trainers to avoid focusing exclusively on gamiﬁcation strate-
gies and, as Corporate Trainer 4 noted, ensure that they “have a good mix of different 
methodologies that ﬁt.” 
These methodologies require a well-structured approach to training and development 
plans. A primary consideration to this includes assessments that must cohesively tie 
to the gamiﬁed experience of participants, regardless of modality. Corporate Trainer 
7 states, “I would consider it as a structured learning experience…one activity and 
then there’s a processing after. And then relating that activity to the bigger concept.” 
Processing activities ensure safeguards that check for understanding. These assessments 
of learning manifest in two distinct ways: through deductive and inductive learning. 
Deductive learning is crucial for information delivered explicitly by trainers. Interview 
6 mentions, “So right now, for us to know [if they understood a topic], we add some 
small assessments, short assessments, just for the topic adjustment.” 
Meanwhile, inductive learning ensures that participants organically absorb the 
intended learnings from sessions, as mentioned also by Corporate Trainer 6: “…the 
meat of most of my activities are done in group works. So, whether it be on site or online, 
they work on case studies, they share personal experiences.” Employing these different

assessments helps ensure training plans are deliberately well-rounded. As such, training 
sessions with cohesive training structures do contribute to positive team engagement. In 
the study, two major group types were distinguished: one for homogenous groups whose 
participants come from the same company, and the other a more heterogeneous mix of 
participants coming from multiple institutions. For heterogeneous groups, it was noted 
that learning should capitalize on the knowledge already acquired by the participants. 
Corporate Trainer 2 exclaims, “…we believe that the knowledge is within the partici-
pant, and we need to draw out that knowledge so that at least they will be aware of it.” 
Corporate Trainer 1 supports this claim, saying that, “[The activity] has to be familiar 
to them..it can’t be overly complicated.” 
Meanwhile, for homogeneous groups, it is crucial to leverage adjacent parts of the 
training management cycle. It is crucial to ﬁnd opportunities to tailor training sessions 
to their organizational context. Corporate Trainer 8 shares, “I would recommend orga-
nizations to start as usual by assessing the speciﬁc needs of their particular workforce, 
and the type of training that will best suit those needs.” Apart from training needs, it is 
also crucial to consider the post-session effectiveness of the training, as mentioned by 
Corporate Trainer 7: “I ask them to have an accountability buddy who they will share 
their commitments with.” and that “The last one would be action planning because 
unfortunately in training sessions it’s just a one-off session, so after that I will no longer 
have visibility on whether they change their learning behaviors.” Measures such as 
using accountability buddies and action planning segments leverage the homogeneity 
of the group. All in all, these ﬁndings suggest that gamiﬁcation plays a crucial role in 
encouraging active employee participation in both onsite and online training settings. 
6 
Recommendations 
With Corporate Trainer 8’s observation that “when participants are able to receive 
instant feedback, it helps them assess their progress and adjust accordingly,” corporate 
trainers should immediately and consistently use quick polls and verbal feedback in 
both online and onsite training to improve self-awareness of their progress and increase 
information retention. Furthermore, Corporate Trainer 2 speciﬁcally emphasized that 
these “breakout rooms in Zoom are very critical” for encouraging group collaboration 
via online engagement. Hence, within the next month, corporate trainers who conduct 
online training programs via platforms like Zoom or Microsoft Teams should maximize 
the use of breakout rooms, with clear tasks and reporting mechanisms to foster peer 
learning and enhance engagement in the virtual environment. Within the next one (1) to 
three (3) months, corporate trainers should integrate interactive gamiﬁcation elements, 
particularly readily available digital tools like Mentimenter, Kahoot, and Google Slides, 
into existing training modules to boost engagement and provide immediate feedback 
on understanding, aiming for higher participation and completion rates—as Corporate 
Trainer 8 expressed, “When I am able to make the program interactive and enjoyable, 
I notice that there’s a higher level of participation and completion rate,” and Corporate 
Trainer 2 mentioned using “Mentimeter, Kahoot, and Google Slides.” Lastly, Corporate 
Trainer 2 explained using “point systems [onsite]…so that at least they’re motivated 
to enjoy the activity,” and Corporate Trainer 7 highlighted that “…the game-like ele-
ments that are most effective would be incentives.” Corporate Trainer 8 also noted that

competition “drives the participants to perform better.” Therefore, corporate trainers 
and organizations’ HR departments should design and pilot a system within two (2) 
months to acknowledge participation and achievement with low-cost rewards, such as 
public acknowledgement, digital badges, or small company-branded items, increasing 
motivation and engagement throughout the training program and fostering a competitive 
yet collaborative learning environment. 
It is recommended that corporate trainers employ a well-structured, gamiﬁed learning 
framework that combines online and onsite training methodologies, given how Corpo-
rate Trainer 4 emphasized that “Training programs must have a good mix of differ-
ent methodologies…” This strategy should carefully align speciﬁc workforce learning 
objectives and performance evaluations with game elements such as leaderboards, point 
systems, and incentives complemented with onsite methodologies such as team building 
and collaborative activities like role playing for better learning outcomes. This should 
be implemented within six (6) to twelve (12) months, with the assistance of instruc-
tional designers, digital platforms, and systematic post-training follow-up mechanisms. 
According to the corporate trainers interviewed, this type of integration promotes infor-
mation retention, motivation, and employee engagement in a variety of ways, especially 
when gamiﬁcation is purposefully planned and supported by collaborative exercises.
